French is widely spoken in Algeria, which makes it unusually easy for francophone Canadians — and our guides work in English and French alike. The French edition of this site is at [algeriacompass.com/fr/](/fr/).

This page gathers what matters when you are travelling from Canada: getting there, the visa, time and money, when to go, and which of our private tours fit best. Every tour is private, on your own dates, with a licensed local guide. Our guides work in English and French.

## Getting there
**Montreal** has long had direct flights to Algiers — around seven hours — thanks to the large Algerian community in Quebec. From Toronto, Vancouver or Calgary, connect through Montreal or through a European hub such as Paris, Frankfurt or Istanbul. Check current schedules when you book.

## The visa
Visitors from Canada need a visa for Algeria. There are two routes:

1. **A tourist visa from the Algerian embassy or consulate** that serves you, applied for before you travel. Consulates want an invitation letter and a confirmed programme from a licensed Algerian operator — we prepare both **free with every booking**.
2. **The Saharan visa-authorisation**, for organised trips that enter Algeria through the south, such as Djanet or Timimoun. We issue the authorisation (**€40**) and the visa sticker is issued on arrival; a separate sticker tax depends on your nationality.

Send us your nationality and travel dates and we will tell you which route applies. The details are on our [entry and e-Visa page](/evisa/) and in our [visa guide](/blog/algeria-visa-guide/).

## Time, power and money
- **Time:** Algeria runs on UTC+1 all year, with no daylight saving. Algiers is **6 hours ahead of Montreal and Toronto** in winter and 5 in summer; **9 hours ahead of Vancouver** in winter and 8 in summer.
- **Power:** Algeria uses 230 V power and **European Type C and F plugs**. Bring an adapter, and check that anything without a dual-voltage label can take 230 V.
- **Money:** Our tour prices are in euros, and you pay us before the trip. On the ground Algeria runs on cash: bring **euros** (or US dollars) in clean notes to change — Canadian dollars are hard to change. Cards are rarely accepted outside big hotels — see [can I use credit cards in Algeria?](/questions/can-i-use-credit-cards-in-algeria/)

## When to go
The Sahara is travelled from **October to April** — the perfect escape from a Canadian winter, with warm desert days (the nights are cold). Spring and autumn suit the north. See our [month-by-month guide](/blog/best-time-to-visit-algeria/).

The north of Algeria, the M'Zab Valley and the organised Sahara routes are calm and very welcoming; our [safety guide](/blog/algeria-safety-guide/) sets out the evidence.
