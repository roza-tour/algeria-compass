For Dutch travellers who have already done Morocco, Algeria is the next step: the same North African warmth, far fewer visitors, and a much bigger and wilder Sahara. Our honest comparison is in [Algeria vs Morocco](/blog/algeria-vs-morocco/).

This page gathers what matters when you are travelling from the Netherlands: getting there, the visa, time and money, when to go, and which of our private tours fit best. Every tour is private, on your own dates, with a licensed local guide. Our guides work in English (and French and German).

## Getting there
Algiers is about **three hours' flying from Amsterdam**. Depending on the season there may be a direct service; otherwise one connection through Paris, Brussels, Frankfurt or Istanbul. Check current schedules when you book.

## The visa
Visitors from the Netherlands need a visa for Algeria. There are two routes:

1. **A tourist visa from the Algerian embassy or consulate** that serves you, applied for before you travel. Consulates want an invitation letter and a confirmed programme from a licensed Algerian operator — we prepare both **free with every booking**.
2. **The Saharan visa-authorisation**, for organised trips that enter Algeria through the south, such as Djanet or Timimoun. We issue the authorisation (**€40**) and the visa sticker is issued on arrival; a separate sticker tax depends on your nationality.

Send us your nationality and travel dates and we will tell you which route applies. The details are on our [entry and e-Visa page](/evisa/) and in our [visa guide](/blog/algeria-visa-guide/).

## Time, power and money
- **Time:** Algeria runs on UTC+1 all year, with no daylight saving: **the same time as the Netherlands in winter**, and one hour behind in summer.
- **Power:** Algeria uses the same **230 V power and Type C and F plugs** as the Netherlands — no adapter needed.
- **Money:** Our prices are in euros, so there is nothing to convert. On the ground Algeria runs on cash: bring **euros** in clean notes to change. Cards are rarely accepted outside big hotels — see [can I use credit cards in Algeria?](/questions/can-i-use-credit-cards-in-algeria/)

## When to go
The Sahara is travelled from **October to April** — **winter sun** three hours from home, with warm desert days and cold, starry nights. Christmas and New Year in the desert are special: see our [winter guide](/blog/christmas-new-year-in-algeria/).

The north of Algeria, the M'Zab Valley and the organised Sahara routes are calm and very welcoming; our [safety guide](/blog/algeria-safety-guide/) sets out the evidence.
