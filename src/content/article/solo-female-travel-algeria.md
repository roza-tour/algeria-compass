Is Algeria a good place for a woman travelling alone? **Yes** — and many women travel the country solo and come home with stories of kindness. Here is how it feels on the ground, told honestly by the people who will be hosting you.

## The overall picture
Algerians treat a guest as someone to look after, and a woman travelling alone often receives an extra share of that care. In the **north and on the usual tourist routes** — Algiers, Constantine, Oran, the Roman cities, the coast — you will be welcomed: a woman on the tram showing you where to get off, a family at the next table sending over a plate of pastries, a shopkeeper who insists you sit down for tea. As a foreign woman you will also draw **attention** (looks, sometimes a comment), mostly because visitors are still rare, and now and then it can tip into an unwelcome remark, as in much of the wider region. Serious incidents are uncommon.

## Dress and demeanour
- Dress **modestly**: cover shoulders and knees, keep a scarf handy. It is appreciated, and it makes the welcome easier.
- Walk with **confidence** and purpose; it keeps unwanted attention at a distance.
- Be friendly, and keep the details of where you are staying and travelling next for people you **know**.

## Getting around
- Use **trusted transport** — official taxis, transfers arranged by your hotel or guide, trains between cities.
- After dark, choose **lively, well-lit streets**.
- Learn a few words of **French or Arabic**; a simple "saha" or "shukran" brings smiles and changes every conversation.

## The Sahara
The deep south is a **guided journey for everyone**, woman or man: a licensed operator, a guide and a 4x4. Within it you become part of a small travelling family — the cook baking taguella bread in the sand, the guide who knows where every rock engraving hides — and you don't travel the deep desert independently.
