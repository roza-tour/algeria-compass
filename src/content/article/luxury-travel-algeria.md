Search for luxury travel in Algeria and you will find very little, which tells you something true about the country and something false about the trip. Algeria has almost no luxury marketing, because it has almost no mass tourism to market against. What it has instead is the thing luxury travel is usually trying to buy back: somewhere extraordinary with nobody in it.

This is an honest account of what the top end of Algerian travel actually is, what it costs, and where it will disappoint you if you arrive with the wrong expectations.

## Where the five-star hotels actually are

The northern cities. Algiers has the widest choice, including international-brand hotels on the bay, and there are comparable properties in Oran and Constantine. In those three cities you can expect the service standard you would expect anywhere.

Move outside them and the market thins quickly. In the oasis towns and the Roman east you are in good, clean, well-run local hotels — comfortable, and not five-star. In the deep Sahara there is no hotel at all. The top tier there is a private serviced camp: proper bedding, a cook, a support vehicle, water, communications and a licensed guide, on authorised routes.

If a hotel bed with a view is the centre of the holiday, Algeria is not the strongest choice in North Africa, and we would rather say so before you book than after.

## What you are actually buying

Exclusivity, access and competence.

**Sites with nobody in them.** Timgad is Trajan's grid-planned colony of AD 100, laid out complete across an open plain. Djémila drapes over a mountain spur with mosaics among the richest in the Roman world. Tipaza runs along the Mediterranean with the waves at the foot of the basilicas. All three are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. On an ordinary day you may share them with a school group and nobody else. That is not a marketing line; it is a consequence of Algeria receiving a fraction of its neighbours' visitors.

**Everything private.** At the top end nothing is shared: your own 4WD fleet, a tour leader who stays with your group from arrival to departure, and an expert local guide at each site. There are no fixed departure dates to fit around, because there are no fixed departures.

**The logistics absorbed.** This is the part that is invisible when it works. Internal flights to the south are thin and fill up. Sahara permits take time and must be applied for by a licensed Algerian operator. Consulates want an invitation letter and a confirmed programme before they will issue a tourist visa. Someone has to do all of that, in-country, in Arabic and French, and get it right.

## The season is not negotiable

The deep Sahara is a winter destination: **November to February**, when the days are warm and clear and the nights cold enough for a fire. December and January are the heart of it. From late spring the desert becomes dangerously hot and expeditions stop — this is a safety limit, not a preference.

The north is comfortable from **October to April**, and spring and autumn are the pick for the Roman cities and the coast. A two-week trip in December can reasonably combine both. See [when to go](/blog/best-time-to-visit-algeria/) for the month-by-month detail.

## Choosing an operator

Algeria requires the operator on the ground to be Algerian and licensed, which means a good many international listings are resold — you buy from one company and are met by another.

Three questions settle it. Who holds the Algerian operating licence? Who physically meets you at the airport? Who applies for your Sahara permits? If the answers name a third party, you are paying a margin for less control, and when a southern flight moves at short notice, control is the whole product.

We are a licensed Algerian operator and answer all three with our own name. If you are comparing us against someone else, ask them the same three questions.

## Is it worth it?

If you want an extraordinary place without the crowd that usually comes with it, and you can accept a visa process, a cash economy and hotels that are comfortable rather than the point, Algeria is one of the best-value serious trips in the Mediterranean world.

If you want a resort, a spa and a booking confirmation in ninety seconds, it is not — and no amount of the word "luxury" on a brochure will change that.
