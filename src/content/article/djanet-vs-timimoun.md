Ask anyone planning a first trip to the Algerian Sahara and the question usually comes down to two names: **Djanet** or **Timimoun**. They are both Saharan, both red, both extraordinary — and they are not the same trip at all. We run tours to both, so here is the straight comparison.

## The short answer
- Go to **Djanet** for the **deep desert**: the sandstone arches and dune seas of the [Tadrart Rouge](/tours/tadrart-rouge-7-days/), the prehistoric rock art of the [Tassili n'Ajjer](/destinations/tassili-najjer/), and nights camped under a sky with no light in it at all.
- Go to **Timimoun** for the **living oasis**: red mud-built ksour, palm gardens fed by thousand-year-old foggara channels, markets and music — and a guesthouse bed every night.

## The landscape
**Djanet** is the Sahara most people picture before they have seen it. Around the town, rose-coloured sand runs up against black and red rock: towers, canyons, natural arches, and the great dune fields of the Tadrart, south-east toward the Libyan border. It is a wilderness, and you travel through it by 4×4, stopping where the landscape tells you to.

**Timimoun** is gentler and more human. The town and the ksour of the Gourara around it are built from the same red earth as the ground, set above palm groves on the edge of the Grand Erg Occidental. The dunes are there — and there is a 4×4 afternoon in them on our trip — but the heart of it is the oasis.

## What you actually see
- **Djanet:** rock engravings and paintings up to 10,000 years old, recording a green Sahara of giraffes, elephants and cattle — the "Crying Cow" of Tigharghart, the giraffes of Tin Abadène, and, for walkers, the painted walls of [Sefar](/tours/sefar-tassili-trek/). And, on the [Ihrir circuit](/tours/ihrir-oasis-7-days/), something nobody expects: permanent pools of water in the middle of the desert.
- **Timimoun:** the ksour of Charouine and Tala, the underground **foggara** channels that still carry water to the gardens, the market, and evenings of traditional music. See the [Timimoun Desert Escape](/tours/timimoun-desert-escape/).

## Comfort and effort
This is where the choice is often made.

- **Timimoun** is **easy to moderate**. You sleep four nights in a guesthouse, go out on excursions and come back.
- **Djanet** by 4×4 is **moderate**: short walks and dune climbs, but most nights are in desert camps, and winter nights are close to freezing. There is no phone signal once you leave town — which is rather the point.
- The **Sefar trek** is **demanding**: four to seven hours of walking a day on the Tassili plateau, with donkeys carrying the camp. It is for regular walkers only.

## Season
Both are **winter destinations**: October to April, with November to February the clearest and coolest. The deep south is not travelled in summer. Read more in our [month-by-month guide](/blog/best-time-to-visit-algeria/).

## So which should you choose?
If you want **the Sahara itself** — emptiness, rock, sand and the oldest art in Africa — choose **Djanet**. If you want the desert **with people in it**, a warm bed and an easier pace, choose **Timimoun**.
