Most people book Christmas somewhere expensive and crowded. Algeria offers the opposite: **warm desert days, empty world-class sites, and no festive price surge** — because in a majority-Muslim country, 25 December is simply a working day.

That is the honest pitch. Here's what a Christmas and New Year trip actually looks like.

## Why late December is a great time to come

**The Sahara is in its prime season.** The deep desert — the [Tassili n'Ajjer](/destinations/tassili-najjer/), the Tadrart, the Hoggar — is only sensible between November and February. Christmas lands squarely in that window: **warm days of roughly 20–25°C**, and cold, exceptionally clear nights. Spending Christmas Eve at a desert camp under that sky is not something you forget.

**The north is mild and green.** While much of Europe is grey and frozen, the Mediterranean coast sits at around **10–17°C**, the winter rains have turned the hills green, and the Roman cities are pleasant to walk.

**The mountains have snow.** The Atlas ranges — **Chréa** above [Blida](/provinces/blida/) and the **Djurdjura** in Kabylie — hold real winter snow, an hour or two from the capital.

**And there are no crowds.** No queues at Timgad. No festive markup on hotels. Sites, transport and tours run their normal schedules.

## What Christmas itself is like

Let's be straight: **Algeria does not "do" Christmas commercially.** You will not find Christmas markets, decorated high streets or festive menus outside a handful of international hotels.

What you *will* find:
- **Church services.** Algeria's small Christian community marks Christmas, and services are held at the **Sacré-Cœur Cathedral** and the hilltop basilica of **Notre-Dame d'Afrique** in Algiers, and in **Oran** and **Constantine**. Notre-Dame d'Afrique is a mainstream visitor site with a magnificent view over the bay, and features on our [Algiers day tour](/tours/algierstour-hammagarden-notredame-dafrique/).
- **Complete normality otherwise** — which, if you are tired of the commercial season, is precisely the appeal.

For the full calendar context, see our [holidays guide](/blog/algeria-holidays-ramadan/).

## New Year — and then Yennayer

**1 January is a public holiday** in Algeria. And on **12 January** comes **Yennayer**, the **Amazigh (Berber) New Year**, also a national holiday, celebrated with traditional meals and family gatherings, strongest in **Kabylie**, the **Aurès** and the **M'Zab**.

A trip running from late December into mid-January can therefore take in **three new years** — the Gregorian, the local celebrations around it, and the Amazigh one.

## A Christmas-week shape that works

A 7–10 day trip over the holidays:

1. **Algiers** (2–3 nights) — the [Casbah](/destinations/casbah-of-algiers/), the seafront, Notre-Dame d'Afrique.
2. **Fly south** to **Djanet** or **Tamanrasset** for a **guided desert leg** (3–4 nights) — dunes, rock art, camps under the stars over Christmas.
3. **Return north** for New Year, or add **Chréa** for snow and cedar forest.

See our [itinerary guide](/blog/algeria-itinerary/) for the longer versions, and our [Sahara guide](/blog/algerian-sahara-travel-guide/) for how desert legs are run.

## Practical notes

- **Pack for both extremes.** Mild and sometimes wet in the north; **desert nights fall close to freezing**. Our [packing list](/blog/algeria-packing-list/) covers it.
- **Start the visa early.** December is a busy period for consulates — see the [visa guide](/blog/algeria-visa-guide/).
- **Book the desert leg ahead.** Winter is high season for the Sahara specifically, and guided capacity is finite.
