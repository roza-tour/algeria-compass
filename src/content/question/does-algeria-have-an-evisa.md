This is one of the most common false starts in planning an Algerian trip. Travellers search for an e-visa, find a commercial site that appears to offer one, pay a fee, and receive nothing that a consulate will accept.

Algeria has **no general electronic tourist visa**. The tourist visa is a consular process: a paper file, submitted to the Algerian embassy or consulate that covers your place of residence, with a physical sticker placed in your passport. Some missions have modernised the front end — online appointment slots, downloadable or partially pre-filled forms, courier return of passports — but the decision and the visa itself remain consular.

Two things follow. First, be sceptical of any website that promises an "Algeria e-visa" for a fee; at best these are form-filling services charging for a free PDF, and the consulate has no obligation to recognise them. Go directly to the official mission for your country. Second, budget real calendar time — see [how long does an Algeria visa take?](/questions/how-long-does-an-algeria-visa-take/) — because there is no same-week online shortcut to fall back on.

The one genuine exception to the advance-visa rule is the organised far-south desert tour, covered in [can I get an Algeria visa on arrival?](/questions/can-i-get-an-algeria-visa-on-arrival/). Everything else runs through the process described in our [Algeria visa guide](/blog/algeria-visa-guide/).
