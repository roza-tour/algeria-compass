It depends what you want from a trip, and the honest version of the answer is more useful than an enthusiastic one.

**Algeria is worth it if** you value places over comfort. You can stand in Timgad — a complete Roman city, a UNESCO site, the "Pompeii of Africa" — on a weekday in winter and see almost nobody. You can spend four days in the Tassili n'Ajjer among prehistoric rock art and sandstone towers with no other group in sight. You will be invited for tea by strangers repeatedly and mean it when you say it was the best part. Prices, once you have dinars, are low.

**Algeria is not worth it if** you want a frictionless holiday. There is a real [visa process](/questions/do-i-need-a-visa-for-algeria/) to complete weeks in advance. It is a [cash economy](/questions/can-i-use-credit-cards-in-algeria/) where your cards will not work. Tourist infrastructure is thin: fewer good hotels outside the main cities, opening hours that are not published, little English, and distances that punish over-ambitious itineraries. It is not a beach-resort destination.

The travellers who come back delighted are the ones who came for the Roman cities, the desert, the architecture or the photography, allowed enough days, and either travelled with an operator or arrived with patience and some French.

The travellers who are disappointed are the ones who expected Morocco with fewer tourists. It is a different proposition — less polished, less commercial, and considerably more surprising.
