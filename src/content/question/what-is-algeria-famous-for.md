Algeria is the **largest country in Africa** and one of the least travelled in the Mediterranean world — which is itself part of what it is known for among people who follow travel closely.

**The Sahara.** Four-fifths of Algeria is desert, and it is not one landscape but many: the sandstone forests and rock art of the **Tassili n'Ajjer**, the red dunes and arches of the **Tadrart**, the volcanic peaks of the **Hoggar** with the hermitage at Assekrem, and the ochre oases of the Gourara around Timimoun.

**Roman Africa.** Because Algeria never developed mass tourism, its Roman cities survived in a state that has largely vanished elsewhere. **Timgad** is a complete grid-planned colony founded by Trajan, **Djémila** sits spectacularly in the hills, and **Tipaza** runs along the Mediterranean shore.

**Seven UNESCO World Heritage Sites**: the Casbah of Algiers, Timgad, Djémila, Tipasa, the M'Zab Valley, Al Qal'a of Beni Hammad and Tassili n'Ajjer.

**Amazigh (Berber) culture**, indigenous and still living — in Kabylie, the Aurès, the M'Zab and among the Tuareg of the deep south, with its own languages, music and craft.

**Music and food.** **Raï** came out of Oran and went global; *chaabi*, *malouf* and *gnawa* are equally alive. **Couscous** is UNESCO-recognised heritage shared across the Maghreb.

And historically: the war of independence, and writers from Augustine of Hippo to Camus and Assia Djebar.
