Algeria is a conservative country with a low violent-crime rate and a deep habit of hospitality, and those facts shape the whole answer. Solo women do travel here — through Algiers, Oran, Constantine, Béjaïa, the Roman sites — and the overwhelming majority come home talking about kindness.

What you should expect is **curiosity**. In smaller towns a foreign woman alone is unusual, so people will look, ask where you are from, and offer help you did not ask for. It can feel like a lot at first, but it comes from interest rather than ill will, it fades in the big cities, and it all but disappears when you are with a guide or a family.

Practical things that make a real difference: dress modestly — shoulders and knees covered, nothing tight; a headscarf is not required outside mosques but many women carry one. Travel between cities in daylight rather than at night. Use ordered taxis or your accommodation's driver rather than flagging cars down. Sit in the back of taxis. Feel free to decline a conversation politely and directly; it is understood.

Above all, Algeria is a country where you will be looked after. Families, shopkeepers and other women will step in if they think you are lost or being bothered, and the woman you asked for directions may well end up inviting you for coffee.

In the deep Sahara everyone travels with a guide, man or woman — [nobody travels the far south independently](/questions/can-i-visit-algeria-without-a-tour-guide/). For the fuller picture, region by region, see the [Algeria safety guide](/blog/algeria-safety-guide/).
