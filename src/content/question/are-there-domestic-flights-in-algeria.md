Algeria has a functioning domestic network and you will almost certainly use it, because the country is too large to cross by road inside a normal holiday.

**Air Algérie** is the national carrier and runs the bulk of the network from Algiers (ALG) to Oran, Constantine, Annaba, Tlemcen, Béchar, Ghardaïa, Adrar, Timimoun, Tamanrasset and Djanet, among others. **Tassili Airlines**, originally the oil-industry operator, adds capacity on southern routes and is often the one flying the desert legs.

For the **north**, flying is optional — Algiers to Oran or Constantine is a comfortable train or a long drive, and the ground journey has its own value. For the **south**, it is not optional in any practical sense. Algiers to Tamanrasset is roughly two thousand kilometres of road; Djanet is further still. Every realistic Sahara itinerary flies down and starts the 4x4 travel from there.

Practical points that catch people out. Southern routes are **thin** — sometimes a few flights a week rather than daily — so they dictate the shape of your itinerary rather than fitting into it, and they fill in the winter desert season. Book early. Schedules can move, so avoid same-day connections onto international flights. And domestic booking from abroad is easier through an agency than on your own card, given [how limited card payment is in Algeria](/questions/can-i-use-credit-cards-in-algeria/).
