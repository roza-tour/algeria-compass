For Muslim travellers, Algeria removes the question entirely. It is an overwhelmingly Muslim country, and **halal is the default rather than the exception**. Butchers, markets, restaurants, street stalls, hotels and airline catering all operate on that basis. There is no separate certification to look for, no halal district to find, and no need to interrogate a menu.

**Pork** is effectively absent from the food supply. It is not part of Algerian cuisine, it is not stocked in ordinary shops, and outside a very small number of specialist outlets serving foreign residents you will not encounter it.

Practical notes for observant travellers. Prayer facilities are everywhere — mosques in every neighbourhood, prayer rooms in airports, shopping centres, service stations and many workplaces — and prayer times structure the working day. During **Ramadan** the rhythm of the whole country changes, which is covered in [can you visit Algeria during Ramadan?](/questions/can-you-visit-algeria-during-ramadan/).

**Alcohol** is a separate matter and does not affect the food: it is legal but discreet, absent from the overwhelming majority of restaurants, and confined to some hotels and licensed venues in the larger cities. See [is alcohol available in Algeria?](/questions/is-alcohol-available-in-algeria/).

For travellers with other dietary requirements the picture is different — vegetarians do reasonably well, vegans less so, which we cover in [can vegetarians eat well in Algeria?](/questions/can-vegetarians-eat-well-in-algeria/). Our [food guide](/food/) covers the cuisine itself.
