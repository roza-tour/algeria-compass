You can legally drive in Algeria as a visitor. Hire companies operate in the main cities and at Algiers airport, your national licence plus an **International Driving Permit** is the normal requirement, and the motorway spine across the north — the East–West Highway — is a genuinely good road.

Whether you *should* is a different question. The considerations that push most visitors towards a driver are: assertive urban traffic, especially in Algiers, where lane discipline is aspirational; signage in Arabic and French; roadside assistance that is informal at best; fuel stations that thin out fast once you leave the northern corridor; police and gendarmerie checkpoints that are routine and courteous but easier with someone who can explain your trip; and parking in old city centres that is a sport rather than a service.

Two firm rules. **Avoid night driving on rural roads** — unlit vehicles, livestock and poor markings make it the single biggest road risk in the country. And understand that the **deep south is closed to independent driving**: the Tassili, Tadrart and Hoggar regions require a licensed operator, an accredited guide, convoy 4x4s and permits, and no hire company will let you take a saloon car down there in any case.

For a trip focused on the north, a car with a driver usually costs less than people assume, removes every one of the frictions above, and turns a transfer into a conversation. See our [transport guide](/blog/algeria-transportation-guide/) for the full comparison.
