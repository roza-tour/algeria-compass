Algeria is a trilingual country, and English is the language it does not run on.

**Arabic** — Modern Standard in official use, Algerian *darija* in the street — and **Tamazight** (Berber, in several regional varieties including Kabyle, Chaoui and Mozabite) are the official languages. Tamazight is dominant in Kabylie, the Aurès and the M'Zab.

**French** is the de facto second language and the one most useful to a visitor. It is on street signs, menus, government forms, product packaging and shop fronts; it is the language of much of business and higher education; and most adults can hold a conversation in it. If you have school French, Algeria is a country where it suddenly becomes valuable.

**English** is a different picture. It is taught in schools, it is spreading fast among people under thirty, and it is standard among professional tourism guides and in international hotels. Outside those contexts — a taxi, a market, a small-town café, a museum ticket desk — you should not assume it.

The practical advice is not to learn a language before you come, but to arrive with a handful of French phrases and a translation app downloaded for offline use. Algerians are famously patient with people making the attempt, and a greeting in Arabic — *salam alaykum*, *shukran* — is received warmly everywhere.
