Algeria is a photographer's country — Roman cities without crowds, white cities against blue sea, the ochre geometry of the M'Zab, and desert light that has no equal. Ordinary travel photography is entirely normal and nobody will stop you.

The **restrictions are narrow but absolute**. Do not photograph military installations or personnel, police or gendarmerie, government and administrative buildings, ports, airports, or the checkpoints you will pass through on intercity roads. These are taken seriously, and a camera raised at a checkpoint turns a thirty-second formality into a long conversation. Put the camera down before you reach one.

**People** are the other consideration, and it is courtesy rather than law. Ask before photographing individuals, and take a refusal gracefully. This matters most with women, where photographing without consent is a real offence to give, and in conservative towns and the M'Zab valley. A moment of asking usually produces a better portrait and sometimes an invitation to tea.

**Sites and museums** vary: some charge a camera fee, some prohibit flash, some prohibit tripods, and a few prohibit photography of specific objects. Ask at the ticket desk rather than assuming.

**Drones** are the one to be careful with. Do not assume you can fly one; import and use are restricted, and turning up with one at customs can go badly. Check well in advance if it matters to your trip.
