Algeria is a conservative country without being a restrictive one for visitors. There is no dress code imposed on foreign women, and you will see Algerian women wearing everything from full traditional dress to jeans, particularly in Algiers, Oran and along the coast. What guides your choice is comfort — social and physical — rather than rules.

The practical standard is **shoulders and knees covered, in loose rather than fitted clothing**. Long trousers or a long skirt with a t-shirt or shirt that covers the upper arm works everywhere in the country and attracts no attention at all. Linen and cotton beat anything synthetic in the heat.

A **headscarf is not required** in the street. It is required to enter a mosque, along with covered arms and legs, so carrying a light scarf in your bag is genuinely useful — it also doubles as sun protection and as an extra layer on cold desert evenings.

**Context shifts things.** Central Algiers and the coastal cities are the most relaxed. Small inland towns, conservative religious centres such as the M'Zab valley, and rural areas warrant more coverage — not because anyone will confront you, but because you will be noticeably more comfortable.

**In the Sahara**, modesty and sun protection point the same way: long loose sleeves and trousers, a scarf for head and face against sun and wind, and warm layers for the night.
