Algeria's railway is a northern story. **SNTF**, the national operator, runs the corridor that follows the country's population: Algiers west to Oran, east to Constantine and Annaba, with branches and regional services in between, plus suburban lines and a metro and tramways in Algiers itself.

The experience is better than most visitors expect. Modern rolling stock runs on the main intercity routes, first class is inexpensive by European standards, and the coastal and highland scenery on the eastern run is genuinely worth the window seat. Journeys are slower than the equivalent flight but far less hassle, and the train is one of the more sociable ways to travel in Algeria — you will be talked to.

What the network does **not** do is go south. The rail map effectively stops at the top of the Sahara. There is freight infrastructure serving mining and industry beyond that, but no passenger service that gets a traveller to Ghardaïa, Timimoun, Tamanrasset or Djanet. Those are reached [by air](/questions/are-there-domestic-flights-in-algeria/), then by 4x4.

Practical notes: buy tickets at the station rather than expecting a working international online booking, travel with your passport, and treat published timetables as accurate but the departure boards as more so. For a short trip, the Algiers–Oran or Algiers–Constantine run is the one worth building in.
