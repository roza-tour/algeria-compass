Algeria runs on the **European standard**: plug types **C and F** — the familiar two round pins, with type F adding earthing clips — at **230 volts, 50 Hz**.

**If you are coming from continental Europe**, your plugs fit and your devices work. Nothing to buy.

**If you are coming from the UK, Ireland, the US, Canada, Australia, Japan or the Gulf**, you need a travel adapter. Buy it before you leave; adapters are not reliably available in Algerian shops in the shapes visitors need.

**Voltage** matters separately from plug shape. Phone, laptop and camera chargers are almost always dual-voltage (check for "100–240V" on the brick) and are fine. Single-voltage 110 V appliances from North America or Japan — some hair dryers, shavers and older electronics — will be damaged without a converter, not merely an adapter.

Two Algeria-specific notes. **Power cuts** happen, particularly in summer and in smaller towns; hotels usually cope, but a charged power bank saves an evening. And in the **Sahara there is no mains supply at all** once you leave the oasis towns. Desert camps run on vehicle inverters and solar at best, with charging rationed and prioritised for the team's satellite and navigation equipment. Bring a large power bank, a solar charger if you shoot a lot, and spare camera batteries — cold desert nights drain them faster than you expect.
