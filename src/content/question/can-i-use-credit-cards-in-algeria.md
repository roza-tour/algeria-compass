Treat Algeria as a **cash country** and you will have no problems. Treat it like Europe and you will be stuck.

Card acceptance exists but is thin and concentrated: some international-brand hotels in Algiers and Oran, airline offices, a few larger businesses. Outside that, everyday life is cash. Restaurants, cafés, taxis, buses, markets, craft shops, museum and site entry, tips, pharmacies — all cash, all in dinars. Domestic card networks exist for residents, but they are not what your Visa or Mastercard will connect to.

ATMs are a related disappointment: they are present in cities, but international-card acceptance is unreliable, withdrawal limits are low, and machines are frequently out of service or out of notes. See [are there ATMs in Algeria?](/questions/are-there-atms-in-algeria/) — the short version is never build your budget on one.

So the plan is: bring your whole trip's spending money as **cash in euros** (dollars second), in good condition, and change it in stages rather than all at once. Keep a card for emergencies and for any hotel that will take one, and tell your bank you are travelling so it is not blocked when you need it.

This sounds inconvenient and is mostly just unfamiliar — once you are carrying dinars, Algeria is inexpensive and easy to spend in.
