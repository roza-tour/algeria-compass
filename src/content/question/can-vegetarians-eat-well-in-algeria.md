Algerian food is more vegetable-friendly than its reputation suggests, but it is not a country with a vegetarian infrastructure.

**What works in your favour:** the cuisine is built on pulses, grains and vegetables. Lentil and chickpea dishes, *loubia* (white bean stew), *hmiss* (roasted pepper and tomato salad), *zaalouk*, brilliant tomato and onion salads, olives, fresh bread, *msemen* and *mhadjeb* flatbreads, and an enormous range of fruit, nuts and dates. Breakfast is naturally meat-free. Pastries and mint tea are a food group.

**What works against you:** meat is the centre of hospitality, and a guest offered a vegetable dish may be read as a guest being poorly treated. More practically, **stock is everywhere** — couscous broth, *chorba* and most soups start with meat, even when no meat is visible in the bowl.

The technique that works is to **name foods rather than the diet**. "I don't eat meat, chicken or fish" is understood; "I'm vegetarian" often is not, and may return with chicken. In a restaurant, ask for a plate of salads, a pulse dish and bread — this is a normal order and usually excellent. On an organised trip, tell your operator before you travel so kitchens are told in advance; that single step changes the experience completely.

**Vegans** will find it harder: butter, eggs, dairy and honey run through the baking and the sweets. It is doable with planning, not by improvisation.
