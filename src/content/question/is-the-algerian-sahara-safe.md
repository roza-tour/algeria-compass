The Algerian Sahara is often talked about as one vast unknown. Those who travel it find something else: silence, campfires and a very old hospitality, where a Tuareg guide welcomes you with the three glasses of tea that open every desert evening.

The **routes** are well known. The classic destinations — the Tassili n'Ajjer and Tadrart around Djanet, the Hoggar and Assekrem around Tamanrasset, the Gourara oases around Timimoun — welcome travellers every winter on permitted routes. The only areas not open to visitors are a few far frontier zones, which no permit and no operator will take you to. See [which parts of Algeria should tourists avoid?](/questions/which-parts-of-algeria-should-tourists-avoid/).

The **desert itself** is what asks for experience, whatever your itinerary. Daytime heat outside winter is fierce. Distances between fuel and water are enormous. Sand and rock call for the right vehicles. Phone coverage fades away. And the tracks are best read by people who have driven them all their lives.

That is why the deep south is travelled as an organised, expedition-style trip: 4x4s equipped and travelling together, drivers who know the routes, water and fuel calculated per vehicle, satellite communications, and permits filed in advance. None of this is paperwork for its own sake; it is how the crew looks after you, so that all you have to do is watch the light change on the dunes.

Travel in the cool season — November to February — with a licensed team, and the Sahara is one of the most rewarding and least crowded landscapes on earth. See our [Sahara region guide](/regions/sahara/) for what the routes actually involve.
