The Algerian Sahara has a fearsome reputation that mixes two very different things: a security question that applies to specific remote frontiers, and an environmental one that applies everywhere.

The **security** part is narrow and well mapped. The classic desert destinations — the Tassili n'Ajjer and Tadrart around Djanet, the Hoggar and Assekrem around Tamanrasset, the Gourara oases around Timimoun — are visited by travellers every winter on permitted routes. The zones that carry real warnings are the far frontier areas, which no permit and no operator will take you to. See [which parts of Algeria should tourists avoid?](/questions/which-parts-of-algeria-should-tourists-avoid/).

The **environmental** part is the one that actually hurts people, and it does not care about your itinerary. Daytime heat outside winter is genuinely lethal. Distances between fuel and water are enormous. Sand and rock terrain destroys unsuitable vehicles. Phone coverage disappears. Navigation errors that would be trivial elsewhere are serious here.

That is why the deep south is an operator-led, expedition-style trip: 4x4s equipped and doubled up, drivers who know the routes, water and fuel calculated per vehicle, satellite communications, and permits filed in advance. Those are not bureaucratic formalities, they are the safety system.

Travel in the cool season — November to February — with a licensed team, and the Sahara is one of the most rewarding and least crowded landscapes on earth. See our [Sahara region guide](/regions/sahara/) for what the routes actually involve.
