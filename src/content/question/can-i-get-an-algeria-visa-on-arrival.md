Algeria is not a visa-on-arrival country in the way Egypt or Jordan are. For virtually every nationality the tourist visa is issued **before departure**, by an Algerian embassy or consulate, and airlines will refuse boarding without it.

The exception that causes most of the confusion is the **organised desert tour**. Groups travelling to the deep south — typically flying into Djanet or Tamanrasset for the Tassili n'Ajjer or the Hoggar — can in some cases be processed on arrival, because a licensed Algerian agency has filed the group's details and permits in advance. This is not something an independent traveller can arrange at the airport; it exists because the agency is accountable for the group.

Two practical consequences. First, if the deep Sahara is your reason for coming, ask your operator early whether your nationality and entry point qualify — it can save several weeks. Second, if you are visiting the north, the coast or the Roman cities, plan on a normal consular application and start it well ahead of your flights.

Requirements change without much notice, so treat any blog post, including this one, as orientation rather than authority: confirm with your nearest Algerian consulate.
