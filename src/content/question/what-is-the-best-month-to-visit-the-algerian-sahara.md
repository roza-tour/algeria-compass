The Algerian Sahara has a season, and it is **winter**. From roughly November to February the desert is at its best: daytime temperatures that are warm rather than punishing, dry clear air, and long low light that transforms the sandstone of the Tadrart and the Tassili.

**December and January** are the heart of it. Expect genuinely cold nights — the desert loses its heat fast after sunset and camps can drop close to freezing, which surprises people who packed only for the daytime. A proper jacket, a hat and layers matter more here than in most winter destinations.

**November and February** are the shoulders and are excellent: slightly warmer nights, marginally fewer travellers, and the same clear conditions. March can still work, and is when the wind picks up.

From **late spring the window closes**. By May daytime heat in the deep south becomes a serious physiological risk, and through the summer it is simply not a place for tourism — operators do not run desert programmes then, and the restriction is a safety judgement rather than a commercial one.

This is the inverse of the northern coast, which is at its best from late spring to early autumn. It is also why a trip that wants both the Mediterranean and the desert works best in the shoulders, and why a winter trip to Algeria is far more rewarding than most people expect.
