There are ATMs across Algerian cities and larger towns, attached to the main banks. The problem is not their existence but their reliability for a foreign visitor.

Three things go wrong. **Acceptance** is inconsistent — the same card can work at one bank's machine and be rejected at the next, and there is no way to know in advance. **Limits** are low, so even a working machine may not dispense what a day of travel costs, and repeated withdrawals stack up fees. **Availability** is variable: machines are out of service, out of notes, or unusable outside branch hours more often than you would expect.

There is also a financial argument even when everything works. An ATM gives you the **official exchange rate**. Arriving with cash and changing it gives you access to the parallel rate, which is materially better — see [what currency does Algeria use?](/questions/what-currency-does-algeria-use/). Over a two-week trip that difference is real money.

The sensible approach is to bring your trip's budget in **euro cash**, change it in stages through your hotel or operator, and treat any ATM you find as a bonus rather than the plan. Keep a card as an emergency backstop and make sure your bank knows you are in Algeria so it is not frozen at the moment you actually need it.

Once you are holding dinars, the country is cheap to travel in. See the [Algeria travel cost guide](/blog/algeria-travel-cost/) for what things actually cost.
