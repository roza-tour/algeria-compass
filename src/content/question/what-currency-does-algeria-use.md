Algeria's currency is the **Algerian dinar**, written DZD or DA. The important thing for a visitor is not the name but the fact that it is a **closed currency**: it is not traded internationally, you cannot order it from your bank at home, and you are not supposed to take meaningful quantities out. You arrive with foreign cash and change it here.

The second important fact is that Algeria effectively has **two exchange rates**. There is the official rate used by banks and the airport bureau, and there is a widely used parallel market rate, which is substantially more favourable to someone arriving with euros. Nearly every visitor encounters this, most local businesses price with it in mind, and the practical difference across a two-week trip is not small. Our [Algeria travel cost guide](/blog/algeria-travel-cost/) explains how this reshapes a budget.

Practical consequences worth planning around: bring **euros** if you can — they are the most widely accepted, ahead of US dollars — in clean, undamaged notes. Bring more cash than instinct says, because [cards are barely usable](/questions/can-i-use-credit-cards-in-algeria/). Declare currency above the threshold on your arrival form, keep the slip, and change money through your hotel or operator rather than an approach on the street.

Prices for everyday things — food, transport, entry tickets — are low by European standards once you are here. It is the getting-hold-of-dinars step that catches people out, not the cost of living.
