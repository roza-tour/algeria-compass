For most travellers arriving from Europe, North America or the Gulf, Algeria has **no mandatory vaccination requirement**. You will not be asked for a vaccination certificate at the border in the ordinary case.

The exception is **yellow fever**: as in much of North Africa, proof of vaccination can be required of travellers arriving from, or having recently transited, a country where yellow fever is endemic. If your route passes through sub-Saharan Africa or parts of South America, check this specifically.

Beyond that, health advice is the standard travel-clinic picture rather than anything Algeria-specific: be up to date on your **routine vaccinations**, and discuss the usual travel additions with a clinician who can weigh them against your itinerary and medical history. Malaria is not a general concern in the areas visitors travel.

Two Algeria-specific practicalities matter more than any injection. First, **bring your own medication** — prescription drugs in original packaging with a copy of the prescription, plus a basic kit including rehydration salts, since pharmacies are good but brand names differ and stock varies. Second, **do not drink the tap water**: see [can you drink the tap water in Algeria?](/questions/can-you-drink-the-tap-water-in-algeria/).

In the Sahara the real health risks are environmental — heat, dehydration and sun — which is why desert programmes carry water by the vehicle-load. See [is the Algerian Sahara safe?](/questions/is-the-algerian-sahara-safe/).

This page is orientation, not medical advice. See a travel clinic six to eight weeks before departure.
