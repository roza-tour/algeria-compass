Algeria is one of the few Mediterranean countries that is arguably **better in winter than in summer**, and the reason is the desert.

The deep Sahara — the Tassili n'Ajjer, the Tadrart, the Hoggar — is only travellable from about November to February. Outside that window the heat makes it unsafe and expeditions do not run. So a winter trip is not a compromise on the desert; it is the *only* version of it.

The rest of the country cooperates. The **Roman cities** — Timgad, Djémila, Tipaza — are exhausting under a July sun and perfect in December: cool, clear and, since Algeria has almost no mass tourism, frequently empty enough that you have Trajan's arch to yourself. The **northern coast** stays mild rather than cold, greener than in summer, with rain rather than heat as the variable. And the **Atlas** genuinely gets snow — there is skiing at Chréa above Blida, and snow on the Djurdjura in Kabylie.

There is one genuinely unusual proposition here: within a single winter fortnight you can be in snow in the Atlas and in warm dunes in the Sahara. Few countries offer that range in one trip.
