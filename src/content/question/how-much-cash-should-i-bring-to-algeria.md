Because [cards barely work](/questions/can-i-use-credit-cards-in-algeria/) and [ATMs are unreliable](/questions/are-there-atms-in-algeria/), the honest planning rule for Algeria is: **bring the whole trip in cash, and bring it in euros.**

Build the number from what you will actually pay on the ground, not from a daily average copied off a generic budget site. Start by subtracting everything already prepaid — on an organised programme that is usually accommodation, transport, guides and most meals, which removes the largest items. What remains is genuinely discretionary: meals not included, café stops, taxis, museum and site entry, tips, souvenirs and craft purchases, a SIM card, and a contingency for a change of plan.

Everyday costs in Algeria are low by European standards, so this total is usually smaller than travellers fear. What matters more is the **margin**: you have no easy way to add funds, so plan comfortably over rather than exactly.

Practical points. Bring **euros** first, dollars second, in clean undamaged notes — torn or heavily marked notes are refused or discounted. Include a mix of denominations. Declare cash above the customs threshold on your arrival form and keep the slip, because it matters when you leave. And change money in stages through your hotel or operator rather than all at once on arrival.
