The short answer is **no** — not because Algerian tap water is untreated, but because of how it reaches you.

Municipal supply in the cities is chlorinated and monitored. The complication is that supply in many areas is **intermittent**, so buildings store water in rooftop or basement tanks and draw from them between deliveries. Tank cleanliness is variable, pipe networks are old in the historic quarters, and the result is water that may be fine and may not, with no way for a visitor to tell.

The practical answer is easy: **bottled water is cheap, excellent and sold absolutely everywhere**, from city supermarkets to the smallest desert village shop. Algeria has good mineral springs and several well-known domestic brands. Use it for drinking and for brushing your teeth, particularly outside the main cities and in your first days while your system adjusts.

Ice, salads and street food are the usual secondary questions. In established restaurants and hotels these are generally fine; the ordinary traveller caution applies more in small roadside places.

**In the Sahara** the question disappears — water is not a local supply issue but a logistics one, carried in bulk by your operator's vehicles and rationed per person per day. Desert programmes plan several litres per person daily, and drinking steadily rather than waiting for thirst is the single most important health habit on a desert trip.

Reusable bottles with a filter work well if you want to cut plastic use, though bottled water remains the default. See our [packing list](/blog/algeria-packing-list/).
