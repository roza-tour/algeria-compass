The answer is reassuringly short. The only parts of Algeria that are not open to visitors are a few **remote southern and south-eastern frontier zones** — the areas near the Mali, Niger and Libyan borders — and stretches of far desert well away from established routes.

They are not places anyone ends up by accident. They lie hundreds of kilometres from any travel itinerary, no permit is issued for them, and no licensed operator goes there.

Everything travellers actually come for is open, and that is where the welcome happens: Algiers and the Casbah, where a shopkeeper may pour you mint tea while you browse; the Mediterranean coast; Oran and the west; Tlemcen; Constantine and the Roman east; Kabylie and its mountain villages; the M'Zab Valley; and the great Saharan circuits around Djanet, Timimoun and Tamanrasset, travelled with an operator, a licensed guide and permits.
