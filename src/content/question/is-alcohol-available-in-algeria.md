Alcohol occupies an unusual position in Algeria: legal, locally produced, and socially discreet all at once.

It is **not prohibited**. Algeria has a real wine industry — the vineyards of the Oran and Mascara region were among the largest in the world under French rule and still produce — and beer and spirits are sold through licensed outlets. What has changed over the decades is visibility rather than legality.

In practice: some **international-standard hotels** in Algiers and Oran serve alcohol to guests, a limited number of **licensed restaurants** have a list, and **specialist shops** exist in the larger cities if you know where they are. Ordinary restaurants and cafés — the overwhelming majority — do not serve alcohol, and there is no bar culture in the European sense.

It thins out fast as you leave the big cities. Conservative inland towns, the M'Zab valley and the Saharan south are effectively dry, and asking around for alcohol there is a social misstep rather than a shopping problem.

The sensible approach for a visitor is to treat Algeria as a country you are not drinking in, and take what you find as a bonus. Nobody will judge a guest who has a glass of wine at their hotel; drinking visibly in public, or during Ramadan, is a different matter — see [can you visit Algeria during Ramadan?](/questions/can-you-visit-algeria-during-ramadan/).

What you will drink constantly, and remember, is **mint tea and strong coffee**, both of which come with an entire hospitality culture attached.
