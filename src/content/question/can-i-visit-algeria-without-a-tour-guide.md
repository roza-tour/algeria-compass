Algeria splits neatly in two on this question.

**The north is open.** Nothing stops you walking the Casbah of Algiers, taking the train to Oran, or spending a day at Tipaza or Djémila on your own. Independent travellers do it, and the country is safe and hospitable for it. What they run into is not restriction but friction: little tourist infrastructure, signage and service overwhelmingly in Arabic and French, limited English, a cash economy, and museums and sites whose opening hours are not reliably published anywhere.

**The deep south is not.** The Tassili n'Ajjer, the Tadrart, the Hoggar and the border regions are travelled with a licensed operator, an accredited guide and permits. This is partly security policy and partly plain physics — these are areas where a breakdown far from a road is a survival problem, not an inconvenience. Vehicles, fuel, water, communications and a driver who knows the terrain are the actual product.

So the honest answer depends on your trip. A week in the north with some French and a tolerance for improvisation is perfectly doable alone. A trip built around the desert, or one that tries to cover both in limited time, is not — and mixing the two independently tends to eat days in transit.
