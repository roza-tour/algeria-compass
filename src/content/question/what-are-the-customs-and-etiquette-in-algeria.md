Algerian social life runs on courtesy, and a visitor who observes a handful of conventions will find doors — sometimes literal ones — opening.

**Greet first.** Walking up to someone and immediately asking a question reads as rude. *Salam alaykum*, a handshake, a moment of how-are-you, and then the question. Men shake hands with men; with women, wait to see if a hand is offered, and a hand on the heart is a warm and always-correct alternative.

**Accept the tea.** Hospitality is not a formality here; it is the point. Being invited for tea or a meal by someone you have just met is normal and sincere, and declining flatly is a small rejection. If you genuinely cannot stay, decline warmly and at length rather than briskly.

**In a home:** remove your shoes at the door unless told otherwise, bring pastries or fruit if invited for a meal, compliment the food, and expect to be fed considerably more than you intended to eat.

**Use your right hand** for eating, and for giving and receiving anything.

**Dress modestly** — see [what should women wear in Algeria?](/questions/what-should-women-wear-in-algeria/); men should avoid shorts outside beaches.

**Religion:** ask before entering a mosque, and expect some to be closed to non-Muslims. Dress fully covered. Prayer times punctuate the day and shops may close briefly.

**Photography:** [ask before photographing people](/questions/is-photography-allowed-in-algeria/).

None of this is a test. Algerians are forgiving of foreigners getting it wrong, and generous with those who try. Our [culture guide](/culture/) goes deeper into the traditions behind these habits.
