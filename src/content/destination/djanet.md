## A living Tuareg oasis
Djanet is not only a doorway to the desert — it is a place in its own right: a **palm oasis** in Algeria's far south-east that is the **cultural capital of the Kel Ajjer Tuareg**. Its gardens, markets, music and the famous **Sebeiba festival** give the deep Sahara its human face. (Planning the trip — flights, permits, seasons — is covered in the [Djanet travel guide](/provinces/djanet/).)

## The Kel Ajjer Tuareg
The people of Djanet are the **Kel Ajjer**, one of the Tuareg confederations of the central Sahara. For centuries their **camel caravans** linked the Mediterranean to the Sahel, and that heritage of mobility, trade and desert knowledge still shapes life in the oasis. The Tuareg are known for a society in which **indigo-veiled men** and a strong matrilineal thread give the culture its distinctive character.

## Language, poetry & music
Djanet speaks **Tamahaq**, the Tuareg branch of Berber, written in the angular **Tifinagh** script that descends from ancient Libyco-Berber writing. It is a culture carried in **oral poetry and song** — sung verse, the one-stringed *imzad* fiddle and rhythmic hand-clapping that fill weddings, festivals and long desert nights.

## Dress & identity
The most recognisable Tuareg image — the **indigo tagelmust**, the veil-turban traditionally worn by men — belongs to this world. The deep blue dye that rubs onto the skin gave the Tuareg their old European nickname, the "blue people." Dress here is identity, status and protection from sun and sand at once.

## The Sebeiba festival
Djanet's signature event is the **Sebeiba**, a ritual of competitive **dance and song** staged between the town's two historic quarters, **El Mihan** and **Zelouaz**. Held over several days, it channels an ancient rivalry into performance rather than conflict — and was inscribed on **UNESCO's Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2014**. It is the clearest expression of Djanet's living culture.

## Crafts, markets & oasis life
Daily life turns around the **palm gardens** and the **market**, where Tuareg **silver jewellery** (the cross-of-Agadez and engraved talismans), leatherwork and woven goods are made and sold. Water from **gueltas** — natural rock pools — feeds the date palms, and the rhythm of the oasis still follows the seasons of the harvest.

## Desert hospitality
Hospitality is central to Tuareg identity: the **three-glass tea ritual**, brewed slowly over charcoal, and a tradition of welcome that has guided travellers across the Sahara for generations. Spending an evening with a Tuareg desert team — tea, stories and navigation by the stars — is for many visitors the heart of a Djanet journey.

## Beyond the oasis
The same desert that shaped this culture holds its two great landscapes: the **[Tassili n'Ajjer](/destinations/tassili-najjer/)**, whose prehistoric rock art was made by the ancestors of today's desert peoples, and the **Tadrart Rouge**, the red dunes and arches south of town. Both are reached from Djanet — see the [Djanet travel guide](/provinces/djanet/) to plan a visit.
