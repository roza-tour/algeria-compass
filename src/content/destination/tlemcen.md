## Overview
**Tlemcen** is Algeria's great repository of **Andalusian** civilisation — the refined urban culture carried across the strait from Muslim Spain. Set in the green, hilly north-west near the Moroccan border, it was the capital of the medieval **Zayyanid** kingdom and a celebrated centre of scholarship, craft and music. It is often, and fairly, called the *Pearl of the Maghreb*. (To plan a visit, see the [Tlemcen travel guide](/provinces/tlemcen/).)

## History — the Zayyanid capital & al-Andalus
Tlemcen rose as the capital of the **Zayyanid (Abd al-Wadid)** dynasty and a hub on the trans-Saharan and Mediterranean trade routes — a rival to Fez across the border. Successive waves of **Andalusi refugees**, Muslims and Jews expelled from Spain, brought the learning, architecture and music of **al-Andalus**, and the city became a celebrated seat of scholarship and Sufism. The ruined royal city of **Mansourah**, raised during a 14th-century Marinid siege, recalls the wars that marked its medieval power.

## The Great Mosque
The **Great Mosque of Tlemcen**, begun under the **Almoravids** in the early 12th century, is one of the masterpieces of western Islamic architecture — its carved cupola and mihrab among the finest in the Maghreb. It stands at the heart of the **old medina**, the city's living historic core.

## Mansourah
On the city's edge stand the haunting ruins of **Mansourah** — the walls and a great **lone minaret** of a rival town built during a Marinid siege of Tlemcen. Half-ruined and free-standing against the sky, the minaret is one of Algeria's most evocative monuments.

## Sidi Boumediene
The hillside complex of **Sidi Boumediene** at **El Eubbad**, built around the tomb of the great Sufi saint, is a jewel of **Hispano-Moorish** woodwork, tile and carved plaster — mosque, madrasa and tomb gathered above the city, and a place of pilgrimage to this day.

## Gharnati — the city's music
Tlemcen is the Algerian guardian of **gharnati**, the strand of classical Andalusian music named for Granada. Performed in the city's houses and festivals and taught across generations, it is the most living of Tlemcen's links to al-Andalus.

## Craft & identity
The city's identity is **urbane, devout and proud of its Andalusian inheritance**, expressed in fine **craft** — embroidery, weaving and woodwork — as much as in its monuments. The **El Mechouar** palace-citadel in the centre, restored to its Zayyanid form, completes the picture of a royal Andalusian capital.
