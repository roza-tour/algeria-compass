## Overview
**Tlemcen** is Algeria's great repository of **Andalusian** civilisation — the refined urban culture carried across the strait from Muslim Spain. Set in the green, hilly north-west near the Moroccan border, it was the capital of the medieval **Zayyanid** kingdom and a celebrated centre of scholarship, craft and music. It is often, and fairly, called the *Pearl of the Maghreb*.

## Heritage
The **Great Mosque of Tlemcen**, begun under the Almoravids in the early 12th century, is one of the masterpieces of western Islamic architecture. On the city's edge stand the haunting ruins of **Mansourah** — the walls and lone minaret of a rival town raised during a 14th-century Marinid siege — while the **Sidi Boumediene** complex at El Eubbad, built around the tomb of the great Sufi saint, is a jewel of Hispano-Moorish woodwork, tile and carved plaster. Tlemcen remains a guardian of classical **Andalusian music**, the gharnati tradition still performed in its houses and festivals.

## What to see
- The **Great Mosque** and the old medina
- **Mansourah** — minaret and ramparts
- **Sidi Boumediene** mosque, tomb and madrasa at El Eubbad
- The **El Mechouar** palace-citadel in the centre
- The **El Ourit** waterfalls and the Lalla Setti plateau above the city

## How to reach
Tlemcen has its own airport and good road links from Oran (around 1.5 hours) and across the west.
