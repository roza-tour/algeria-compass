## Why visit Tipaza
Tipaza is where ancient Algeria meets the sea. A short drive west of Algiers, this small coastal state wraps some of the most beautifully set Roman ruins in the Mediterranean around a working fishing town and a string of beaches. You can walk among temples and bathhouses with the waves a few metres away, climb to a royal tomb in the hills, and eat fresh fish by the harbour — all in a single, easy day from the capital.

## A short history
Founded by the **Phoenicians** as a trading post, Tipaza grew into a significant **Roman** colony and an early centre of North African Christianity, leaving behind basilicas as well as temples. Inland stands the older **Royal Mausoleum of Mauretania**, linked by tradition to **Juba II and Cleopatra Selene**, while along the coast lies **Cherchell** — Roman **Caesarea** — once a royal capital and now home to a fine archaeological museum. The seaside ruins were inscribed by **UNESCO in 1982**, and famously moved the writer **Albert Camus**, who set an essay here.

## What to see
The **archaeological park** of Tipaza holds the forum, two basilicas, an amphitheatre, baths and villas strung along low cliffs. A short distance inland, the monumental **Royal Mausoleum** commands the hills, and the **Cherchell museum** displays Roman statuary and mosaics gathered from the ancient capital. Between the sites, the coast offers harbours, coves and beaches.

## Food and the coast
This is **seafood** country: grilled fish and shellfish straight off the boats, eaten beside the water. The summer months bring Algerois families to the beaches, and the fishing harbours are at their liveliest in the morning.

## Getting there and around
Tipaza is about **70 km west of Algiers**, roughly **1.5 hours** by road, and is most often visited as a **day trip** from the capital — frequently combined with the Royal Mausoleum and Cherchell. **Spring and autumn** are ideal for the ruins; **summer** suits the beaches. We arrange a licensed guide and transport from Algiers.

## Practical tips
- Combine the **ruins, the Royal Mausoleum and Cherchell** in one well-paced day.
- Bring **sun protection and water** — the seaside site is open and exposed.
- **Spring and autumn** give the best light and the fewest crowds at the ruins.
