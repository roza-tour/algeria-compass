Snow to Sahara: Four Seasons in One Algeria Trip

July 2026 · 9 min read

Written by Oussama Ben Yahia Reviewed by Algeria Compass Editorial Review Last reviewed July 22, 2026 Updated July 22, 2026
Quick answer

Yes, it snows in Algeria — the Atlas mountains, including Chréa above Blida, the Djurdjura in Kabylie and the Aurès, get real winter snow, and Chréa and Tikjda have small ski stations. Because Algeria is the largest country in Africa, its climates stack from the Mediterranean coast through mountains and high plateaus to the Sahara, so a single winter trip can take you from snow to warm desert dunes in a matter of hours by road and a short domestic flight.

Snow in the cedars, sun on the dunes

Key takeaways

  • Algeria genuinely gets snow — the Atlas ranges hold it through mid-winter.
  • Chréa (above Blida) and Tikjda (Djurdjura) have small ski stations near the capital.
  • The country stacks four climate zones: coast, mountains, high plateaus, Sahara.
  • In one winter week you can see snow and warm desert dunes.
  • The contrast is a winter-only trick — in spring the snow is gone, in summer the desert is off-limits.

Most visitors picture Algeria as one thing: sand. It is the largest country in Africa, and its climates stack — Mediterranean coast, forested mountains, cold high plateaus, then the Sahara. In winter that stacking produces a genuinely unusual holiday: you can stand in snow and, days later, in warm desert dunes.

Yes, it snows in Algeria

Every winter, the Atlas ranges hold real snow:

  • Chréa, in the cedar forests above Blida — roughly an hour from Algiers, with a small ski station.
  • Tikjda, in the Djurdjura mountains of Kabylie — mountain walking, snow and another modest ski station.
  • The Aurès, above Batna — snowy peaks near the Roman city of Timgad.

The high plateaus around Sétif and Batna are also properly cold in winter, with occasional snow in the towns themselves.

These are modest facilities, not alpine resorts — but the snow, the cedars and the emptiness are real.

How to build the snow-to-Sahara week

The contrast only works December to February, when the mountain snow and the prime Saharan season overlap.

  1. Algiers (2 nights) — coast, the Casbah, mild sea air.
  2. Chréa (day trip or 1 night) — cedar forest and snow, about an hour inland.
  3. Fly south to Djanet or Tamanrasset (3–4 nights) — guided 4x4, dunes, rock art, camps under an exceptional winter sky.
  4. Optional: the Roman east (Timgad, Djémila) on cold, clear plateau days.

Fly the southern leg — the road distances are enormous. Our transport guide explains the logistics, and the itinerary guide has full-length plans.

Packing for two climates at once

This is the part people get wrong. In the same bag you need:

  • Mountain-cold layers — proper warm clothing, waterproof outer, decent shoes.
  • Desert kit — strong sun protection by day, and warm layers again for freezing desert nights.

Full checklist in our packing list.

When it doesn’t work

  • Spring — the snow has largely gone; lovely for the north, no contrast.
  • Summer — the deep desert is dangerously hot and effectively off-limits.

So this is a winter-only trip — which pairs neatly with Christmas and New Year, when the desert is at its best anyway.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming Algeria is all desertThe north is Mediterranean and mountainous — it snows every winter.
  • Trying the snow-to-Sahara contrast in springDo it December–February, when the snow is there and the desert is in season.
  • Driving to the deep south to save moneyFly the long southern leg; the distances are enormous.
  • Packing for one climateBring mountain-cold layers and desert sun protection in the same bag.

Where this takes you

highlands

Al Qal'a of Beni Hammad

Al Qal'a of Beni Hammad is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in the Hodna mountains of M'Sila state — the ruined first capital of the Hammadid dynasty, founded in 1007. Set at around 1,000 m, it preserves the largest mosque minaret in Algeria and the remains of palaces and fortifications, a vivid picture of a fortified Islamic city of the 11th century.
coast

The Casbah of Algiers

The Casbah of Algiers is the city's historic Ottoman citadel and old town — a steep maze of whitewashed houses, palaces, mosques and covered lanes overlooking the bay. A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1992, it is best explored on foot with a local guide.
highlands

Constantine

Constantine is the 'City of Bridges', a dramatic highland city in north-east Algeria built across a deep gorge above the Rhumel River and spanned by spectacular bridges. Ancient Cirta — a Numidian and Roman capital and later an Ottoman seat — it is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities on earth and the home of malouf music.

Frequently asked questions

Does it snow in Algeria?

Yes. The Atlas mountains get real winter snow, including Chréa above Blida, the Djurdjura range around Tikjda in Kabylie, and the Aurès mountains. Snow typically falls between December and March, and the high plateaus around Sétif and Batna also see cold, occasionally snowy winters.

Can you ski in Algeria?

There are small ski stations at Chréa, in the mountains above Blida about an hour from Algiers, and at Tikjda in the Djurdjura. They are modest facilities rather than alpine resorts, but skiing and snow days are genuinely possible in a good winter.

Can you see snow and the Sahara on the same trip?

Yes, in winter. Because Algeria stacks Mediterranean coast, mountains, high plateaus and desert, you can walk in cedar-forest snow near Blida and, after a short domestic flight south, stand among warm Saharan dunes within the same week.

What is the best month for the snow-and-Sahara contrast?

December to February. That window overlaps the mountain snow season with the prime Saharan season, when desert days are warm and nights are clear and cold. By spring the snow has mostly gone; by summer the deep desert is unsafe.

How cold does it get in Algeria?

Mountain areas fall to around or below freezing in winter, with snow. The northern coast stays mild at roughly 10–17°C. Saharan days are warm, often 20–25°C, but desert nights can drop close to freezing, which surprises most visitors.
