Christmas & New Year in Algeria: A Warm Winter Escape

July 2026 · 10 min read

Written by Oussama Ben Yahia Reviewed by Algeria Compass Editorial Review Last reviewed July 22, 2026 Updated July 22, 2026
Quick answer

Christmas and New Year is one of the best windows to visit Algeria. Late December falls in the prime Sahara season, when desert days are warm and the nights are clear for stargazing, while the Mediterranean north stays mild and green and the Atlas mountains hold snow. Because Christmas is not a public holiday in Algeria, there are no festive crowds and no holiday price surges — sites and tours run normally. New Year's Day is a public holiday, and Yennayer, the Amazigh New Year, follows on 12 January, so a late-December trip can take in three celebrations.

Christmas & New Year

A warm, quiet Christmas — and three new years

Prime Sahara season, a mild green north, snow in the Atlas, and no festive crowds or price surges. Christmas, New Year and Yennayer in one trip window.

Key takeaways

  • Late December is the prime season for the Algerian Sahara — warm days, cold starry nights.
  • No festive crowds and no Christmas price surge, because 25 December is a normal working day.
  • The north stays mild and green while Europe is grey; the mountains hold snow.
  • Churches in Algiers, Oran and Constantine hold Christmas services for those who want them.
  • New Year's Day is a public holiday, and Yennayer (12 January) follows soon after.
  • Desert nights are genuinely cold — pack proper warm layers.

Most people book Christmas somewhere expensive and crowded. Algeria offers the opposite: warm desert days, empty world-class sites, and no festive price surge — because in a majority-Muslim country, 25 December is simply a working day.

That is the honest pitch. Here’s what a Christmas and New Year trip actually looks like.

Why late December is a great time to come

The Sahara is in its prime season. The deep desert — the Tassili n’Ajjer, the Tadrart, the Hoggar — is only sensible between November and February. Christmas lands squarely in that window: warm days of roughly 20–25°C, and cold, exceptionally clear nights. Spending Christmas Eve at a desert camp under that sky is not something you forget.

The north is mild and green. While much of Europe is grey and frozen, the Mediterranean coast sits at around 10–17°C, the winter rains have turned the hills green, and the Roman cities are pleasant to walk.

The mountains have snow. The Atlas ranges — Chréa above Blida and the Djurdjura in Kabylie — hold real winter snow, an hour or two from the capital.

And there are no crowds. No queues at Timgad. No festive markup on hotels. Sites, transport and tours run their normal schedules.

What Christmas itself is like

Let’s be straight: Algeria does not “do” Christmas commercially. You will not find Christmas markets, decorated high streets or festive menus outside a handful of international hotels.

What you will find:

  • Church services. Algeria’s small Christian community marks Christmas, and services are held at the Sacré-Cœur Cathedral and the hilltop basilica of Notre-Dame d’Afrique in Algiers, and in Oran and Constantine. Notre-Dame d’Afrique is a mainstream visitor site with a magnificent view over the bay, and features on our Algiers day tour.
  • Complete normality otherwise — which, if you are tired of the commercial season, is precisely the appeal.

For the full calendar context, see our holidays guide.

New Year — and then Yennayer

1 January is a public holiday in Algeria. And on 12 January comes Yennayer, the Amazigh (Berber) New Year, also a national holiday, celebrated with traditional meals and family gatherings, strongest in Kabylie, the Aurès and the M’Zab.

A trip running from late December into mid-January can therefore take in three new years — the Gregorian, the local celebrations around it, and the Amazigh one.

A Christmas-week shape that works

A 7–10 day trip over the holidays:

  1. Algiers (2–3 nights) — the Casbah, the seafront, Notre-Dame d’Afrique.
  2. Fly south to Djanet or Tamanrasset for a guided desert leg (3–4 nights) — dunes, rock art, camps under the stars over Christmas.
  3. Return north for New Year, or add Chréa for snow and cedar forest.

See our itinerary guide for the longer versions, and our Sahara guide for how desert legs are run.

Practical notes

  • Pack for both extremes. Mild and sometimes wet in the north; desert nights fall close to freezing. Our packing list covers it.
  • Start the visa early. December is a busy period for consulates — see the visa guide.
  • Book the desert leg ahead. Winter is high season for the Sahara specifically, and guided capacity is finite.

Where this takes you

highlands

Al Qal'a of Beni Hammad

Al Qal'a of Beni Hammad is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in the Hodna mountains of M'Sila state — the ruined first capital of the Hammadid dynasty, founded in 1007. Set at around 1,000 m, it preserves the largest mosque minaret in Algeria and the remains of palaces and fortifications, a vivid picture of a fortified Islamic city of the 11th century.
coast

The Casbah of Algiers

The Casbah of Algiers is the city's historic Ottoman citadel and old town — a steep maze of whitewashed houses, palaces, mosques and covered lanes overlooking the bay. A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1992, it is best explored on foot with a local guide.
highlands

Constantine

Constantine is the 'City of Bridges', a dramatic highland city in north-east Algeria built across a deep gorge above the Rhumel River and spanned by spectacular bridges. Ancient Cirta — a Numidian and Roman capital and later an Ottoman seat — it is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities on earth and the home of malouf music.

Frequently asked questions

Can you spend Christmas in Algeria?

Yes, and it is a rewarding time to go. Christmas is not a public holiday in Algeria, so everything operates normally and there are no festive crowds or price surges. Late December is also the prime season for the Sahara, with warm days and clear nights, while the north stays mild. Churches in Algiers, Oran and Constantine hold Christmas services for visitors who want to attend.

Is Algeria a good winter sun destination?

For desert travel, yes. December and January bring warm daytime temperatures in the Sahara — often 20–25°C — with cold, clear nights, while the Mediterranean north stays mild and green at roughly 10–17°C. It is not a beach-swimming destination in winter.

What is the weather like in Algeria in December?

The northern coast is mild and fairly wet, around 10–17°C. The Atlas mountains can be snowy. The Sahara is dry with warm days, often 20–25°C, and cold nights that can fall near freezing.

Are there Christmas celebrations in Algeria?

Not publicly, in the commercial sense — there are no Christmas markets, and 25 December is a working day. Algeria's small Christian community marks it with church services, including at the Sacré-Cœur Cathedral and Notre-Dame d'Afrique in Algiers, and in Oran and Constantine.

Is New Year a holiday in Algeria?

Yes, 1 January is a public holiday. Yennayer, the Amazigh New Year, is also a national holiday on 12 January, so a trip spanning late December into January can take in both.

What should I pack for Algeria at Christmas?

Layers for a mild, sometimes wet north, and genuinely warm clothing for desert nights, which can drop close to freezing. Add sun protection for bright desert days and sturdy shoes for mountain or dune walking.
