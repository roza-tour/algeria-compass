Ramadan, Eid & Holidays in Algeria: A Traveller's Guide

July 2026 · 11 min read

Written by Oussama Ben Yahia Reviewed by Algeria Compass Editorial Review Last reviewed July 22, 2026 Updated July 22, 2026
Quick answer

Algeria's public holidays follow two calendars: fixed national dates (New Year, Yennayer on 12 January, Labour Day, Independence Day on 5 July, Revolution Day on 1 November) and Islamic dates that shift about 11 days earlier each year (Ramadan, Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, the Islamic New Year, Ashura and Mawlid). Travelling during Ramadan is rewarding but changes daily rhythms — many cafés close by day and cities come alive after sunset. Christmas and Easter are not public holidays in Algeria, though small Christian communities mark them in churches in Algiers, Oran and Constantine.

Ramadan & the Algerian calendar

A month of fasting, lanterns and late nights

Ramadan reshapes the rhythm of the day across Algeria: quiet afternoons, then cities alive after sunset for iftar. Here is what changes, and how to travel through it respectfully.

Key takeaways

  • Islamic holidays move about 11 days earlier each Gregorian year and are confirmed by moon sighting, so always check the current year's dates.
  • Ramadan is a wonderful time to visit culturally, but daytime dining is limited and hours change.
  • The two Eids are the biggest family holidays — expect closures and heavy domestic travel.
  • Yennayer (12 January) is the Amazigh New Year and a national holiday.
  • Friday is the main day of prayer; Friday–Saturday is the Algerian weekend.
  • Christmas and Easter are not public holidays, but churches in Algiers, Oran and Constantine hold services.

Algeria runs on two calendars at once, and knowing which one your trip lands on changes the experience. National holidays fall on fixed Gregorian dates; religious holidays follow the Islamic lunar calendar, which moves roughly 11 days earlier each year and is confirmed by moon sighting. That means the dates below are patterns, not a fixed timetable — always check the current year before booking.

Yennayer is worth knowing about: it celebrates the Amazigh new year and is a national holiday, marked with traditional meals and family gatherings — strongest in Kabylie, the Aurès and the M’Zab. See our culture page for the wider Amazigh context.

The Islamic calendar

These shift each year:

  • Ramadan — the month of fasting from dawn to sunset.
  • Eid al-Fitr (Aïd el-Fitr) — the celebration ending Ramadan; one of the two biggest holidays.
  • Eid al-Adha (Aïd el-Adha) — the feast of sacrifice; the other major holiday.
  • Awal Muharram — the Islamic New Year.
  • Ashura — a day of fasting and remembrance.
  • Mawlid an-Nabawi (El Mawlid) — the Prophet’s birthday.

Travelling during Ramadan

Ramadan is, honestly, one of the most rewarding times to see Algeria — and one that needs a little preparation.

What changes:

  • Many cafés and restaurants close during daylight; hotels usually still serve guests.
  • Opening hours shift — offices and some sites work shorter mornings.
  • Cities become quiet in the late afternoon, then come alive after sunset.
  • Iftar (the breaking of the fast) is a warm, social occasion, and hospitality peaks.

How to travel respectfully:

  • You are not expected to fast, but eat and drink discreetly in public during daylight.
  • Dress modestly — see our packing list.
  • If you’re invited to an iftar, accept: it’s one of the finest experiences the country offers.

Tours and sites generally keep running, so a Ramadan trip is entirely workable — it simply follows a different rhythm.

The two Eids

Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha are the country’s biggest family holidays. Expect:

  • Shops and offices closed for one to two days.
  • Special morning prayers and large family gatherings.
  • Heavy domestic travel — transport and hotels fill up.

If your itinerary is tight, plan around the Eid days rather than through them, and book transport early. Our transport guide covers the logistics.

Christmas, Easter and Christian observance

Algeria is a majority-Muslim country, and Christmas and Easter are not public holidays — 25 December is a normal working day, and you should not expect closures or festive decoration outside a few hotels catering to international guests.

There is, however, a small Christian community, and churches hold services. The most visible are in Algiers — the Sacré-Cœur Cathedral and the hilltop basilica of Notre-Dame d’Afrique above the bay — with further churches in Oran and Constantine. Notre-Dame d’Afrique is a mainstream visitor site year-round and features on our Algiers day tour.

Visitors of any faith are treated with courtesy; Algeria’s religious heritage — Islamic, Christian and Jewish — is layered and old, which is part of what makes it interesting.

The working week

The Algerian weekend is Friday and Saturday. Friday is the main day of congregational prayer, so mornings are quiet and many businesses close. Plan administrative tasks and business meetings for Sunday to Thursday.

Where this takes you

highlands

Al Qal'a of Beni Hammad

Al Qal'a of Beni Hammad is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in the Hodna mountains of M'Sila state — the ruined first capital of the Hammadid dynasty, founded in 1007. Set at around 1,000 m, it preserves the largest mosque minaret in Algeria and the remains of palaces and fortifications, a vivid picture of a fortified Islamic city of the 11th century.
coast

The Casbah of Algiers

The Casbah of Algiers is the city's historic Ottoman citadel and old town — a steep maze of whitewashed houses, palaces, mosques and covered lanes overlooking the bay. A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1992, it is best explored on foot with a local guide.
highlands

Constantine

Constantine is the 'City of Bridges', a dramatic highland city in north-east Algeria built across a deep gorge above the Rhumel River and spanned by spectacular bridges. Ancient Cirta — a Numidian and Roman capital and later an Ottoman seat — it is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities on earth and the home of malouf music.

Frequently asked questions

Can you travel to Algeria during Ramadan?

Yes, and it can be a memorable time to visit. Sites and tours generally operate, though opening hours shift and many cafés and restaurants close during daylight. Cities become lively after sunset with iftar, and hospitality is at its warmest. Travellers are not expected to fast, but eating and drinking discreetly in public during the day is the respectful approach.

What are the public holidays in Algeria?

Fixed national holidays are New Year's Day (1 January), Yennayer, the Amazigh New Year (12 January), Labour Day (1 May), Independence Day (5 July) and Revolution Day (1 November). Islamic holidays — Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, the Islamic New Year, Ashura and Mawlid — follow the lunar calendar and move each year.

Is Christmas celebrated in Algeria?

Christmas is not a public holiday in Algeria, which is a majority-Muslim country, and 25 December is a normal working day. Small Christian communities do mark it, and churches such as the Sacré-Cœur Cathedral and Notre-Dame d'Afrique in Algiers, as well as churches in Oran and Constantine, hold services.

What happens during Eid in Algeria?

Eid al-Fitr, at the end of Ramadan, and Eid al-Adha are the two biggest family holidays. Expect widespread closures of shops and offices for one to two days, special morning prayers, family gatherings and heavy domestic travel. Book transport and accommodation well ahead if your trip overlaps.

What is Yennayer?

Yennayer is the Amazigh (Berber) New Year, marked on 12 January and recognised as a national public holiday in Algeria. It is celebrated with traditional meals and family gatherings, and is especially prominent in Kabylie, the Aurès and the M'Zab.

Should I avoid visiting Algeria during religious holidays?

Not necessarily. Ramadan and the Eids offer real cultural depth, and tours still run. The practical trade-offs are shifting opening hours, reduced daytime dining during Ramadan, and closures plus busy transport around the Eids. If your itinerary is tight, plan around the Eid days.
