    How Much Does a Trip to Algeria Cost? (2026 Budget Guide)

    July 2026 · 12 min read

    Written by Oussama Ben Yahia Reviewed by Algeria Compass Editorial Review Last reviewed July 22, 2026 Updated July 22, 2026
    Quick answer

    Algeria is inexpensive by European standards for food and local transport, mid-priced for comfortable hotels, and pricier for guided Saharan expeditions. A mid-range traveller in the north spends roughly $60–120 a day excluding international flights; a guided deep-desert trip is the big-ticket item at around $150–300+ per day. The single biggest variable is the exchange rate: Algeria has an official rate and a widely-used parallel rate, and changing euros or dollars locally stretches a budget significantly.

    Key takeaways

    • Food and local transport are cheap; comfortable hotels and guided desert trips are where the money goes.
    • Carry cash. Card acceptance is limited outside big-city hotels, and ATMs can be unreliable.
    • The parallel exchange rate for euros/dollars is much better than the official one and materially changes your budget.
    • The Sahara is the single biggest cost — guides, 4x4s, permits and internal flights add up.
    • International flights are usually your largest single expense; the on-the-ground trip is comparatively affordable.

    Algeria has a reputation for being hard to read on price, mostly because of one quirk: the country has an official exchange rate and a widely-used parallel rate, and the gap between them is large. Understand that, carry cash, and Algeria turns out to be an affordable destination for everything except the deep Sahara.

    Here’s what a trip actually costs in 2026.

    The short answer

    • Food and local transport are cheap. A good restaurant meal is often under $10; intercity trains and buses are a few dollars.
    • Hotels are mid-priced. Comfortable rooms in cities run $50–90; budget guesthouses less.
    • The Sahara is the expensive part. Guided desert expeditions — with 4x4s, licensed guides, permits and internal flights — are the big-ticket item.

    A mid-range traveller in the north spends roughly $60–120 a day excluding international flights. A guided deep-desert trip is a different order of magnitude, around $150–300+ a day.

    Where the money actually goes

    The cost table above breaks down the main line items. Three things dominate a budget:

    1. International flights — usually your single largest expense, and entirely dependent on where you fly from.
    2. The Sahara — if you go deep south, this is the costly chapter. It’s not expensive by expedition standards, but it dwarfs the northern part of a trip.
    3. How you change money — the parallel rate for euros and dollars is materially better than the official rate, and it changes what your budget is worth on the ground.

    Cash, cards and the exchange rate

    Algeria is a cash economy for travellers. Card acceptance is limited outside big-city hotels, and ATMs can be unreliable, so:

    • Carry dinars for day-to-day spending.
    • Bring some euros or dollars to change — often the best value.
    • Learn the current rates before you travel; see our complete travel guide for the wider practical picture.

    Sample daily budgets (north, excl. international flights)

    • Budget: ~$30–50 — guesthouses, local food, trains and buses.
    • Mid-range: ~$60–120 — comfortable hotels, the odd private transfer, a day guide.
    • Higher-end: $150+ — best hotels, private driver-guide, domestic flights.

    For the Sahara, budget the guided expedition separately — it’s arranged as its own package, and it’s where careful planning saves the most.

    Where this takes you

    highlands

    Al Qal'a of Beni Hammad

    Al Qal'a of Beni Hammad is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in the Hodna mountains of M'Sila state — the ruined first capital of the Hammadid dynasty, founded in 1007. Set at around 1,000 m, it preserves the largest mosque minaret in Algeria and the remains of palaces and fortifications, a vivid picture of a fortified Islamic city of the 11th century.
    coast

    The Casbah of Algiers

    The Casbah of Algiers is the city's historic Ottoman citadel and old town — a steep maze of whitewashed houses, palaces, mosques and covered lanes overlooking the bay. A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1992, it is best explored on foot with a local guide.
    highlands

    Constantine

    Constantine is the 'City of Bridges', a dramatic highland city in north-east Algeria built across a deep gorge above the Rhumel River and spanned by spectacular bridges. Ancient Cirta — a Numidian and Roman capital and later an Ottoman seat — it is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities on earth and the home of malouf music.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is Algeria expensive to travel?

    No, not for food and local transport, which are cheap by European standards. Comfortable hotels are mid-priced and guided Saharan trips are the expensive part. A mid-range traveller in the north spends about $60–120 a day excluding international flights.

    How much money do you need per day in Algeria?

    Budget travellers in the north can manage on roughly $30–50 a day; mid-range travellers spend about $60–120. A guided deep-desert expedition runs far higher — around $150–300+ a day once guides, 4x4s and permits are included.

    Should I use cash or card in Algeria?

    Carry cash. Card acceptance is limited outside major-city hotels and ATMs can be unreliable, so bring dinars and some euros or dollars to change.

    Why does the exchange rate matter so much in Algeria?

    Algeria has an official exchange rate and a widely-used parallel rate, and the difference is significant. Changing hard currency locally can stretch a travel budget considerably, so it's worth understanding before you arrive.

    What is the most expensive part of an Algeria trip?

    After international flights, the guided Sahara is the biggest cost, because it involves licensed guides, 4x4 transport, permits and internal flights. The northern part of a trip is comparatively affordable.
