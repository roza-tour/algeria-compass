Algeria Itinerary: How to Plan 7, 10 & 14 Days (2026)

July 2026 · 14 min read

Written by Oussama Ben Yahia Reviewed by Algeria Compass Editorial Review Last reviewed July 22, 2026 Updated July 22, 2026
Quick answer

Plan around one or two regions, not the whole country. A focused week covers Algiers, the coast and the eastern Roman cities; 10 days adds either the west (Tlemcen/Oran) or a first taste of the northern Sahara; 14 days lets you combine the Roman north with a proper deep-desert leg (Djanet or the Tassili). The deep Sahara is a winter trip (November–February); the north is best in spring and autumn.

Key takeaways

  • Distances are huge — Algeria is the largest country in Africa — so pick one or two regions and travel them well.
  • A week is enough for a focused northern trip; 10–14 days is the sweet spot for a first visit.
  • The Sahara is a separate, winter, guided experience — build it in deliberately, don't tack it on.
  • Fly for the long legs (Algiers–Djanet, Algiers–Tamanrasset); drive or take the train in the north.
  • Spring and autumn suit the north and west; November–February is for the deep desert.

Algeria is the largest country in Africa, and the single most common planning mistake is treating it as one destination. It isn’t. A good Algeria itinerary picks one or two regions and travels them properly, rather than sprinting between the Mediterranean coast and the deep Sahara — which are as far apart as London and the middle of the desert suggests.

Below are three itineraries that work, built around how people actually travel here: the north and east are the easiest place to start, the west adds Andalusian heritage, and the far south is a guided, winter, fly-in expedition.

How long do you need?

  • A week is enough for a focused northern trip — Algiers, a stretch of coast, and the eastern Roman cities.
  • Ten days lets you add one more region: the west (Tlemcen and Oran) or the M’Zab valley oasis towns.
  • Two weeks is the sweet spot for a first visit that combines the Roman north with a real Sahara leg.

7 days — Northern essentials

A first trip on limited time. Best in spring or autumn.

  1. Algiers (2 nights) — the Casbah (UNESCO), the seafront, the Bardo and the Martyrs’ Memorial.
  2. Tipaza (day trip) — Roman ruins on the coast, an easy half-day from the capital.
  3. Constantine (2 nights) — the city of bridges and gorges, and a base for the east.
  4. Timgad & Djémila (day trips) — two of the best-preserved Roman cities anywhere, with almost no crowds.
  5. Return via Sétif to Algiers.

This route is all reachable by train, road and short domestic hops, and needs no special permits.

10 days — North plus one add-on

Take the 7-day northern core, then choose one of these:

  • The Andalusian west — fly or drive to Tlemcen (Almohad and Zianid heritage) and Oran (coast, culture, the raï city).
  • The northern Sahara — fly to Ghardaïa for the M’Zab valley, five fortified oasis towns that influenced modern architecture.

Both add a distinct second chapter without over-stretching the logistics.

14 days — North and deep Sahara

The complete first visit. Do the Roman north in the first week, then fly south for a guided desert leg in the November–February window:

  • Djanet — gateway to the Tassili n’Ajjer, a plateau of prehistoric rock art and sandstone forests (UNESCO).
  • or Tamanrasset — the Hoggar mountains and the Assekrem sunrise.

The southern legs are arranged with a licensed operator: you’ll need a 4x4, a guide and permits, and internal flights rather than the long desert roads.

Getting the logistics right

  • Fly the long legs. Algiers–Djanet and Algiers–Tamanrasset are internal flights, not drives. In the north, trains and roads are fine.
  • Match the season. The north and west are best in spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November). The deep desert is a winter destination.
  • Start the paperwork early. Most visitors need a visa arranged in advance, and southern permits are handled through your operator — see our visa guide and best-time-to-visit guide.

Who it suits

  • ●●●History & ruins travellersThe eastern Roman circuit (Timgad, Djémila, Constantine) is world-class.
  • ●●●Desert & adventure travellersDjanet and the Tassili n'Ajjer reward the 14-day plan.
  • ●●○First-time visitorsStart in the north; it has the most infrastructure and the easiest logistics.
  • ●○○Independent backpackersFine in the north; the south needs a licensed guide and permits.

Common mistakes

  • Trying to see north and deep south in a single weekGive the Sahara its own multi-day block, or save it for a 14-day trip.
  • Driving the long southern legs to save moneyFly Algiers–Djanet/Tamanrasset; the road distances are enormous.
  • Visiting the Sahara in spring or summerKeep the deep desert to November–February.
  • Leaving the visa and desert permits lateStart both weeks ahead; the south needs agency support.

Where this takes you

highlands

Al Qal'a of Beni Hammad

Al Qal'a of Beni Hammad is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in the Hodna mountains of M'Sila state — the ruined first capital of the Hammadid dynasty, founded in 1007. Set at around 1,000 m, it preserves the largest mosque minaret in Algeria and the remains of palaces and fortifications, a vivid picture of a fortified Islamic city of the 11th century.
coast

The Casbah of Algiers

The Casbah of Algiers is the city's historic Ottoman citadel and old town — a steep maze of whitewashed houses, palaces, mosques and covered lanes overlooking the bay. A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1992, it is best explored on foot with a local guide.
highlands

Constantine

Constantine is the 'City of Bridges', a dramatic highland city in north-east Algeria built across a deep gorge above the Rhumel River and spanned by spectacular bridges. Ancient Cirta — a Numidian and Roman capital and later an Ottoman seat — it is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities on earth and the home of malouf music.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Algeria?

Allow 10–14 days for a first trip that combines the Roman north with one desert region. A focused week is enough if you stay in the north around Algiers and the eastern Roman cities.

Is 7 days enough for Algeria?

Yes, for one region. A week comfortably covers Algiers, a stretch of coast and the eastern Roman cities (Constantine, Timgad, Djémila). It is not enough to add the deep Sahara.

What is the best 10-day Algeria itinerary?

Spend the first half in and around Algiers and the eastern Roman circuit, then use the second half for either the Andalusian west (Tlemcen and Oran) or the M'Zab valley oasis towns, depending on your interests.

Can you visit the Sahara and the north in one trip?

Yes, on a 14-day itinerary: do the Roman north first, then fly south to Djanet or Tamanrasset for a guided 4x4 leg in the Tassili n'Ajjer or Hoggar. Keep it to the November–February window.

Do you need a guide for an Algeria itinerary?

Not in the north, where you can travel independently with patience. The deep south requires a licensed guide, 4x4 transport and permits, so those legs are arranged through an operator.
