Quick answer

Constantine is a highland state in north-eastern Algeria built around one of the most dramatic city sites in the world — a rock plateau split by the deep Rhumel gorge and stitched together by soaring bridges. Ancient Cirta, a Numidian and Roman capital, it is known as the 'City of Bridges', with the Ahmed Bey Palace, the Cirta Museum and a strong tradition of malouf music.

Why visit Constantine

Few cities have a setting like Constantine. The old town stands on a high rock almost ringed by the Rhumel gorge, a ravine that plunges hundreds of metres, and the city leaps across it on a series of spectacular bridges. The result is one of the most theatrical urban landscapes anywhere — and beneath it lies one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities on Earth.

A short history

As Cirta, this was the capital of Numidia under kings like Massinissa, then a wealthy Roman city. Destroyed and rebuilt in the 4th century by the emperor Constantine, who gave it his name, it later became a major Ottoman stronghold, ruled by beys whose palaces still survive. Its layered past — Numidian, Roman, Islamic and Ottoman — is written into its streets and museums.

What to see

The Sidi M’Cid suspension bridge and the other crossings over the gorge are the signature experience, with viewpoints along the ravine. The Ahmed Bey Palace is among the finest Ottoman-era residences in Algeria, and the Cirta Museum holds Numidian and Roman antiquities. The old medina, the Emir Abdelkader Mosque and the Monument aux Morts viewpoint complete the picture.

Culture

Constantine is a great centre of malouf, a classical Andalusian musical tradition carried east from Spain via the cities, and a city of scholarship and religious learning. Its identity is urbane, conservative and proud.

Food

Eastern Algerian cooking, known for refined dishes and pastries, with the city famous for its sweets and its take on classic Algerian grills and stews.

Getting there and around

Constantine has a major airport and sits on the eastern rail and road network, about 1.5–2 hours from Sétif and well placed for the Roman north-east. Spring and autumn are the most comfortable seasons on the highlands. We arrange a licensed guide and transport, often combining Constantine with Timgad, Djémila and Sétif.

Practical tips

  • Walk several of the bridges and find the gorge viewpoints for the full effect.
  • Allow time for the Ahmed Bey Palace and the Cirta Museum.
  • Pair Constantine with the Roman cities of the east for a strong two-to-three-day loop.

Things to see in & around Constantine

roman

Djémila (Cuicul)

Djémila, ancient Cuicul, is one of the best-preserved Roman cities in the world and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1982. Set on a mountain spur in Sétif state, it preserves temples, forums, a theatre, the Arch of Caracalla and outstanding mosaics.
roman

Timgad

Timgad (ancient Thamugadi) is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in Batna state, eastern Algeria — a Roman colony founded around 100 AD by Trajan and the most complete example of the Roman grid-plan city anywhere. Set on the edge of the Aurès mountains, its arch, theatre, forum and famous library survive in open country, earning it the nickname 'the Pompeii of Africa'.
roman

Tipaza

Tipaza is a UNESCO World Heritage Site on the Mediterranean coast about 70 km west of Algiers — a Phoenician trading post that became an important Roman port, its forum, basilicas, theatre and villas spread along the sea. Inland stands the monumental Royal Mausoleum of Mauretania, and the Roman capital of Caesarea (modern Cherchell) lies just along the coast.

Nearby states

roman

Sétif

Sétif is a highland state in north-eastern Algeria, best known for Djémila — one of the world's best-preserved Roman cities and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1982. It sits at around 1,100 m on the High Plateaus, has a cool four-season climate, the cedar forests of Babor, the landmark Ain El Fouara fountain, and a defining place in Algeria's road to independence.
roman

Tipaza

Tipaza is a coastal state on the Mediterranean just west of Algiers, best known for its UNESCO World Heritage Roman ruins set right on the sea. It pairs ancient sites — Tipaza's archaeological park, the Royal Mausoleum of Mauretania and the Roman capital of Cherchell — with beaches, fishing harbours and an easy day-trip distance from the capital.
coast

Algiers

Algiers (El Djazaïr) is Algeria's capital and largest city — a Mediterranean port famous for its UNESCO-listed Ottoman Casbah, white tiered waterfront, French-colonial boulevards, the vast new Great Mosque of Algiers, and grand monuments like Notre-Dame d'Afrique and the Maqam Echahid. It's the country's main gateway and a city best explored on foot, with a guide for the Casbah.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Constantine called the City of Bridges?

Because the old city sits on a rock plateau almost encircled by the deep Rhumel gorge, and a series of dramatic bridges — most famously the Sidi M'Cid suspension bridge — connect it across the ravine.

What is the history of Constantine?

It is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world. As Cirta it was the capital of Numidia and an important Roman city; it was rebuilt by the emperor Constantine, who gave it its modern name, and later flourished under the Ottomans.

What should I see in Constantine?

The bridges and gorge, the richly decorated Ahmed Bey Palace, the Cirta (Gustave Mercier) Museum, the old medina and the Emir Abdelkader Mosque, plus the panoramic viewpoints over the ravine.

How do I get to Constantine?

Constantine has a major airport with domestic and some international flights and sits on the eastern rail and road network, about 1.5–2 hours from Sétif.
