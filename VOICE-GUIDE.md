# Algeria Compass — Editorial Voice Guide

The voice of Algeria Compass is that of a knowledgeable, trusted guide — a well-read friend who knows Algeria intimately and explains it clearly. It is never a salesperson.

## Voice in one line
Knowledgeable, trustworthy and curious — sophisticated but plain-spoken, culturally respectful and historically aware, always practical, never promotional.

## The nine attributes

1. **Knowledgeable** — Write from understanding. Give context, not just labels. Explain *why* a place matters.
2. **Trustworthy** — State what is known, attribute what needs attributing, and flag uncertainty honestly. Never overstate.
3. **Curious** — Lead with genuine interest and discovery. Invite the reader to look closer.
4. **Exploratory** — Frame Algeria as a place to discover and understand, not a product to consume.
5. **Sophisticated** — Assume an intelligent reader. Avoid clichés, hype and filler.
6. **Culturally respectful** — Treat peoples, faiths and traditions with care and accuracy. No exoticising, no stereotypes, no "undiscovered paradise" tropes.
7. **Historically aware** — Place things in time — Numidian, Roman, Islamic, Ottoman, colonial, independence — without lecturing.
8. **Practical** — Pair knowledge with accurate, usable guidance: seasons, access, what to expect.
9. **Authoritative** — Calm confidence earned by research and review, not by adjectives.

## Never
- Never sales-oriented ("book now", "limited offer", "best price", "don't miss out").
- Never promotional or brochure-like ("breathtaking", "paradise", "hidden gem", "bucket-list", "must-see" as filler).
- Never generic tourism marketing or empty superlatives.
- Never hype a place beyond what is true; never invent praise, credentials or reviews.
- Never exoticise or speak *for* Algerians; foreground real history and local context.

## Do
- Lead with substance: what it is, why it matters, what to know.
- Prefer specific, concrete detail over adjectives ("a near-vertical maze of Ottoman alleys" beats "stunning old town").
- Use the active, the precise, and the plain.
- Cite when it counts; link to the relevant guide, hub or source.
- Respect the reader's intelligence and time.

## Tone by surface
- **Guides / provinces / destinations:** rich, contextual, practical. Editorial, like a good guidebook chapter.
- **Knowledge hubs & entity blocks:** clear, structured, factual — built for fast human and machine understanding.
- **Q&A:** direct and useful; answer first, context second.
- **Trust / editorial pages:** plain, candid, institutional.
- **Travel-planning CTAs:** quiet and helpful, offered as a service — never a hard sell.

## Worked examples

| Off-brand (avoid) | On-brand (use) |
|---|---|
| "Book your dream Algerian adventure today!" | "Plan a journey through Algeria — when you're ready." |
| "Djémila is a breathtaking must-see hidden gem." | "Djémila is one of the best-preserved Roman cities in the world, set on a remote upland in Sétif." |
| "Unbeatable prices on Sahara tours!" | "Multi-day Tassili and Tadrart itineraries, with what each route actually includes." |
| "Discover the magic of mysterious Algeria." | "Understand Algeria — its history, landscapes and cultures — before you go." |

## House style
- British-leaning English, consistent within a page.
- Diacritics on Algerian place names where standard (Sétif, Béjaïa, Ghardaïa, M'Zab, Tassili n'Ajjer).
- First mention: full proper name; thereafter, the common short form.
- Numbers and dates: spell out where it reads better; be exact with facts.
- Brand: "Algeria Compass" (never "Algeria Compass Tours" or any agency suffix).
