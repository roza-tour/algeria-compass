    Five fast-moving days across Algeria's headline sights — the Casbah, Roman Tipaza, Constantine, Timgad, Djémila and a desert taste at Bou Saada.

    Overview

    For travellers short on time but long on curiosity, this intensive five days hits Algeria's greatest sights: the Casbah, Bardo Museum and Martyrs' Memorial in Algiers; the Roman coast at Tipaza and Cherchell; Constantine's bridges; the UNESCO Roman cities of Timgad and Djémila; and a desert excursion at Bou Saada. It moves briskly, with a licensed guide and all meals, to pack the country's icons into one trip.

    Day-by-day itinerary

    1. On to Algiers & the Central Coast
    2. Day 1 · Algiers

      Algiers city tour

      Airport welcome and transfer, then the Bardo National Museum, a walk through the UNESCO Casbah, and the Martyrs' Memorial with its view over the capital.

      • The Bardo Museum — Prehistory and ethnography inside a graceful Moorish villa, from Saharan rock-art casts to silver jewellery and everyday Algeria.
      • The Casbah of Algiers — The UNESCO-listed Ottoman citadel — a steep white labyrinth of palaces, mosques and courtyard houses climbing above the bay.
      • The Martyrs' Memorial (Maqam Echahid) — Three soaring concrete palm-fronds raised in 1982 over the city, honouring the dead of the war of independence.
    3. On to Roman Algeria
    4. Day 2 · Roman Algeria

      Tipaza & Cherchell

      West along the coast to the Roman ruins of Tipaza and the Royal Mausoleum of Mauretania, then Cherchell's museum and old harbour.

      • Roman Tipaza — A UNESCO-listed Roman port strung along the Mediterranean — basilicas, baths and a theatre with the waves at their feet.
      • The Royal Mausoleum of Mauretania — A great stone drum on a hill above the sea — the 1st-century-BC tomb of Berber king Juba II and Cleopatra Selene, daughter of Cleopatra of Egypt.
      • Cherchell (ancient Caesarea) — Juba II's old capital, whose museum holds some of Algeria's finest Roman and Greek mosaics and statuary.
    5. Day 3 · Constantine → Roman Algeria

      Constantine & Timgad

      East to Constantine — its suspension bridges and Ahmed Bey Palace — and on to the Roman city of Timgad.

      • Constantine's bridges — The 'City of Bridges' spanning the dizzying Rhumel gorge, the Sidi M'Cid footbridge slung high above it.
      • Ahmed Bey Palace — The early-19th-century Ottoman palace of the last bey, with painted galleries and shaded courtyards.
      • Timgad — Trajan's grid-planned colony of AD 100 — the 'Pompeii of the Aurès', laid out across the open plain.
    6. Day 4 · Roman Algeria

      Djémila & Sétif

      The UNESCO Roman city of Djémila with its forum, theatre and mosaics, then the Aïn El Fouara fountain at Sétif.

      • Djémila (Cuicul) — A UNESCO Roman city in green mountains, superbly preserved — forum, temples and a steep theatre.
      • The Djémila mosaics — On-site mosaics among the richest in the Roman world, in the site museum.
      • Aïn El Fouara, Sétif — The beloved 1898 fountain at the city's heart, Sétif's enduring symbol.
    7. On to Bou Saâda & the High Plateaus
    8. Day 5 · Bou Saâda

      Bou Saada & return

      South to Bou Saada, gateway to the Sahara, for its oasis and a desert excursion among the dunes, before the road back to Algiers.

      • Bou Saada oasis — The gateway to the Sahara, with a desert excursion among the dunes.
      • The Moulin Ferrero — The old mill on the Oued Bou Saada beside the gorge and the palm gardens that Étienne Dinet painted.
      • The dunes south of Bou Saada — A short excursion onto the sand — the first real Sahara on the road south, and the turning point of the day.

      Then the road back to Algiers.
