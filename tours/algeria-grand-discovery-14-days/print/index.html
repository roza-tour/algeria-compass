Private tour itinerary

Algeria Grand Discovery — 14 Days, Coast to Sahara

14 days · 13 nights · From €1,850 per person

Most people choose between Roman Algeria and the Sahara. Fourteen days is what it takes to stop choosing. Private trip on your own dates, with a licensed local guide.

Day by day

Day 1Arrival in Algiers → night flight to Djanet

Arrival at Houari Boumediene Airport, met by our representative, with a day-use room to freshen up. An afternoon orientation walk — Martyrs’ Square, the Grande Poste, the seafront — then an early dinner and the transfer to the domestic terminal for the direct night flight south.

  • Houari Boumediene Airport — Met by our representative, with a day-use room to shower and change after the international flight.
  • Martyrs’ Square and the Grande Poste — The square at the foot of the Casbah and the 1910 neo-Moorish post office — the two landmarks of central Algiers.
  • The seafront arcades — The long white colonnade above the harbour, the image of Algiers seen from the sea, before the transfer for the night flight south.

Day 2Tigharghart & the Tadrart Rouge

Meet your licensed Tuareg guide and head out by 4×4 into the Tadrart Rouge, in the Tassili n’Ajjer National Park. Tigharghart, home of the world-famous "Crying Cow" engraving, then on through giant red dunes and sandstone formations to the deep-desert camp. Traditional Tuareg dinner under the stars.

  • Tigharghart — The "Crying Cow" — the most famous engraving in the Sahara.
  • The Tadrart Rouge — Giant red dunes and sandstone towers inside the Tassili n’Ajjer National Park, crossed by 4×4 with a licensed Tuareg guide.
  • The deep-desert camp — A traditional Tuareg dinner cooked on the fire, and the first night out under the stars.

Day 3The heart of the Tadrart

A full day among the great landscapes of the Tadrart Rouge: El Berdj, Moul Naga and the Erg Tin Merzouga, with prehistoric engravings and panoramic viewpoints along the way. Sunset in the middle of the Sahara and a second night of wild camping.

  • El Berdj — Deep sandstone gorges whose walls carry both engravings and painted panels.
  • Moul Naga — Majestic dune country in the middle of the Tadrart, and the day’s panoramic viewpoints.
  • Erg Tin Merzouga — The great dunes of the Tadrart at sunset.

Day 4In Djaren, Tin Abadène & Ouanahare → Djanet

Deeper into the park: the In Djaren valley, the giraffes and elephants engraved at Tin Abadène, Ouanahare, the Tamezguida "cathedral" and the natural arches. Back to Djanet after sunset.

  • The In Djaren valley and its arches — A dry valley of monumental wind-cut sandstone arches — the Tadrart’s signature formations.
  • Tin Abadène — Prehistoric giraffes and elephants.
  • Tamezguida — A sandstone formation that earns the name "cathedral".

Day 5Djanet → night flight to Constantine

A morning of Tuareg Djanet: the Tassili Museum — the only Tuareg museum in Algeria — the old ksar of Azellouaz and the local market, with free time for souvenirs. A farewell-to-the-Sahara dinner, then the late transfer to Tiska Airport.

  • The Tassili Museum — The only Tuareg museum in Algeria — rock-art records, jewellery, weapons and the daily life of the Kel Ajjer.
  • The ksar of Azellouaz — Djanet’s oldest quarter, a mud-brick village stacked on the rock above the palmeries.
  • Djanet market — The oasis market for Tuareg silver, leather and dates, with free time for souvenirs before the farewell dinner.

Day 6Constantine, City of Bridges

A relaxed late start after the night flight, then the city: the Palace of Ahmed Bey, the Cirta National Museum, the Sidi M’Cid suspension bridge and the Mellah Slimane footbridge, the Place des Martyrs and the old town, and the viewpoints over the Rhumel gorge.

  • Sidi M’Cid Bridge — Slung high above the Rhumel gorge — the signature image of Constantine.
  • Ahmed Bey Palace — The painted galleries and courtyards of the last Bey.
  • The Cirta National Museum and the old town — Constantine’s archaeology and fine-art collection, the Place des Martyrs and the viewpoints over the Rhumel gorge.

Day 7Timgad

A full-day excursion to Timgad, the Roman city Trajan founded for army veterans and the sand preserved almost intact: the Arch of Trajan, the theatre, the forum and capitol, the baths and the grid of streets that made it a textbook of Roman planning.

  • Timgad — The "Pompeii of Africa" — a perfect Roman grid, 115 km from Constantine.
  • The Arch of Trajan — The three-bay arch standing over the decumanus — the image Timgad is known by worldwide.
  • The theatre, forum and baths — A 3,500-seat theatre cut into the slope, the capitol and forum at the centre, and the bath complexes around the grid.

Day 8Constantine → Annaba

Morning drive to Annaba, one of Algeria’s oldest Mediterranean cities: the Basilica of Saint Augustine, the Roman ruins of Hippo Regius, the Seybouse seafront and the corniche, and a lively city centre.

  • The Basilica of Saint Augustine — The 19th-century basilica on the hill above the Roman city, holding a relic of Augustine’s arm.
  • Hippo Regius — Where Augustine was bishop — the Roman city beneath the basilica.
  • The corniche and Cours de la Révolution — Annaba’s seafront drive and the plane-shaded central promenade where the whole city meets in the evening.

Day 9Annaba → Djémila → Algiers

An early start west along the motorway to Djémila: the forum, basilica, temples, triumphal arch and the mosaic museum of ancient Cuicul, 900 m up on its mountain spur. Then on to Algiers for the evening.

  • Djémila — One of the best-preserved Roman cities in the world, and among the most beautifully sited.
  • The Arch of Caracalla — The triumphal arch of AD 216 at the edge of Cuicul’s new forum, 900 m up on its mountain spur.
  • The Djémila mosaic museum — On-site mosaics among the richest in the Roman world, kept beside the houses they were lifted from.

Day 10Algiers in full

A full day in the capital: the UNESCO-listed Casbah, the Bardo National Museum, the basilica of Notre Dame d’Afrique, the Maqam Echahid and the panoramic viewpoints over the Bay of Algiers.

  • The Casbah — The Ottoman citadel above the bay — stepped lanes, courtyards and rooftops.
  • The Bardo National Museum — Prehistory and ethnography in a Moorish villa — Saharan rock-art casts, silver jewellery and everyday Algeria.
  • Notre-Dame d’Afrique and the Maqam Echahid — The basilica on its cliff above Bab El Oued, and the three concrete palm-fronds of the Martyrs’ Memorial over the bay.

Day 11Tipaza & Cherchell → night flight to Ghardaïa

West along the coast to the Roman site of Tipaza, the Royal Mausoleum of Mauretania and the archaeological museum at Cherchell. Back to Algiers for dinner, then the airport.

  • Tipaza — A Roman port city spread along the sea, where Camus walked among the ruins.
  • The Royal Mausoleum of Mauretania — A great stone drum on the hill above the sea — the tomb of Juba II and Cleopatra Selene, daughter of Cleopatra of Egypt.
  • The Cherchell Archaeological Museum — Ancient Caesarea’s collection of Roman and Greek mosaics and statuary, among Algeria’s finest.

Day 12The M’Zab Valley

A full day among the five fortified ksour of the M’Zab: Ghardaïa, Beni Isguen, Melika, Bounoura and El Atteuf, with the traditional market and the Mozabite architecture whose logic fascinated Le Corbusier.

  • Ghardaïa’s market and ksar — The arcaded market square at the foot of the main town, and the tiered houses climbing to the minaret above it.
  • Beni Isguen — The walled holy city of the Ibadi Mozabites.
  • El Atteuf — The oldest of the five towns.

Day 13The Sebseb dunes → evening flight to Algiers

A morning excursion south to the Sebseb dunes — a last taste of the great Sahara — then back to Ghardaïa. Farewell dinner of traditional Algerian cooking before the evening flight north.

  • The road south through the hamada — The stone desert between Ghardaïa and the sand — the transition almost no visitor sees.
  • Sebseb — Dunes 90 km south of Ghardaïa, and the trip’s last desert horizon.
  • A farewell dinner in Ghardaïa — Traditional Algerian cooking on the last night, before the evening flight north.

Day 14Departure

Breakfast, a free morning for anything missed or any last shopping, then the private transfer to Houari Boumediene Airport.

  • A free morning in Algiers — Whatever the fortnight missed — the Jardin d’Essai, the Ketchaoua Mosque, or simply the seafront.
  • Last shopping — Kabyle silver, Tuareg leather, Deglet Nour dates and pastries, on Rue Didouche Mourad or in the Casbah workshops.
  • Houari Boumediene Airport — The private transfer out and assistance through check-in for your international flight.
