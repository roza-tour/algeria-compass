Tadrart Rouge — 7 Days in the Red Sahara

7 days · 6 nights · From €920 per person

The Tadrart is the Sahara people picture before they have seen it: rose-coloured sand running up against black rock, arches the size of cathedrals, and a sky with nothing in the way. Private trip on your own dates, with a licensed local guide.

Day by day

Day 1Djanet → Tigharghart

Land at Djanet after the flight from Algiers and meet the team that will be with you all week. Straight out by 4×4 to Tigharghart, where the first bivouac goes up and dinner is cooked over the fire. First night under tent or, if you prefer, simply under the sky.

  • Djanet — The Tuareg oasis capital of the south-east and the gateway to the Tassili n’Ajjer.
  • Tigharghart — The first camp, at the edge of the rock-art country you will return to on the last morning.
  • The first bivouac — Camp goes up at Tigharghart — mats, a fire, dinner cooked on it, and the choice between a tent and simply the open sky.

Day 2Tigharghart → El Berdj

Breakfast at camp, then a crossing into the gorges of El Berdj — deep-cut walls of sandstone with engravings and paintings along them. Picnic lunch in the shade, camp pitched at El Berdj, and sunset from the dunes above it.

  • The crossing to El Berdj — A morning of 4×4 between the two camps, alternating soft sand and black rock.
  • El Berdj — Impressive gorges whose walls carry both engravings and painted panels.
  • The dunes above El Berdj — The climb up for sunset over the gorge system, with camp pitched at their foot.

Day 3El Berdj → Moul Naga

A day of landscapes that alternate between sand and rock, stopping at several notable rock-art sites along the way. Picnic lunch, then on to Moul Naga, where camp is set among majestic dunes and dinner is eaten under the stars.

  • Sand-and-rock country — A day that alternates dune fields with sandstone massifs — the mix that gives the Tadrart its character.
  • Rock-art stops along the route — Several engraved and painted panels visited on the way: cattle, hunters and the wildlife of a Sahara that had rivers.
  • Moul Naga — A bivouac pitched in the middle of the dunes.

Day 4Moul Naga → Erg Tin Merzouga

Walking in the rose-coloured dunes and the emblematic scenery of the Tadrart Rouge, then arrival at Erg Tin Merzouga — the great sand sea. Climb the high dunes for a sunset that is the reason many people come, and camp at their foot.

  • A morning in the rose dunes — On foot through the pink sand and wind-cut rock that give the Tadrart Rouge its name.
  • Erg Tin Merzouga — The great dune field of the Tadrart, and its most famous sunset.
  • The high dune at sunset — The climb most people come for — the whole erg turning red, then violet, from the top.

Day 5Tin Merzouga → Tissetka → Ouanahar

Down to the Oued In Djaren and on to Tin Abadène, whose engravings show giraffes and elephants — animals from a Sahara that was green. Picnic lunch, then the road to Ouanahar and the evening camp.

  • Oued In Djaren — A dry valley threaded between sandstone walls, and the way down out of the erg.
  • Tin Abadène — Engraved giraffes and elephants, from the era when this desert held rivers.
  • Ouanahar — The night’s camp, deeper into the Tadrart.

Day 6Ouanahar → Ajelati → Oued In Djaren

A morning among monumental natural arches, then the Tamezguida "cathedral" — a sandstone formation that earns the name. A walk in the Oued In Djaren, picnic lunch, and camp at Ajelati for sunset over the black dunes.

  • The natural arches — A morning among the Tadrart’s monumental wind-cut arches — the most photographed formations in the park.
  • Tamezguida — The sandstone "cathedral" of the Tadrart.
  • Ajelati — Black dunes, and the last night in the desert.

Day 7Ajelati → Tigharghart → Djanet → home

Back to Tigharghart for the two engravings everyone comes to see: the "Crying Cow" and the Elephant Rock. Return to Djanet, then the transfer to the airport for the flight north.

  • Tigharghart — the "Crying Cow" — The Sahara’s most photographed engraving, a weeping bovine carved into a low rock face thousands of years ago.
  • The Elephant Rock — Tigharghart’s second famous engraving, a few minutes’ walk from the first.
  • Djanet and Tiska Airport — Back to the oasis for a wash and a last meal before the transfer for the flight north.
