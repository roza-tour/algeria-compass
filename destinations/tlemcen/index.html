Overview

Tlemcen is Algeria’s great repository of Andalusian civilisation — the refined urban culture carried across the strait from Muslim Spain. Set in the green, hilly north-west near the Moroccan border, it was the capital of the medieval Zayyanid kingdom and a celebrated centre of scholarship, craft and music. It is often, and fairly, called the Pearl of the Maghreb. (To plan a visit, see the Tlemcen travel guide.)

History — the Zayyanid capital & al-Andalus

Tlemcen rose as the capital of the Zayyanid (Abd al-Wadid) dynasty and a hub on the trans-Saharan and Mediterranean trade routes — a rival to Fez across the border. Successive waves of Andalusi refugees, Muslims and Jews expelled from Spain, brought the learning, architecture and music of al-Andalus, and the city became a celebrated seat of scholarship and Sufism. The ruined royal city of Mansourah, raised during a 14th-century Marinid siege, recalls the wars that marked its medieval power.

The Great Mosque

The Great Mosque of Tlemcen, begun under the Almoravids in the early 12th century, is one of the masterpieces of western Islamic architecture — its carved cupola and mihrab among the finest in the Maghreb. It stands at the heart of the old medina, the city’s living historic core.

Mansourah

On the city’s edge stand the haunting ruins of Mansourah — the walls and a great lone minaret of a rival town built during a Marinid siege of Tlemcen. Half-ruined and free-standing against the sky, the minaret is one of Algeria’s most evocative monuments.

Sidi Boumediene

The hillside complex of Sidi Boumediene at El Eubbad, built around the tomb of the great Sufi saint, is a jewel of Hispano-Moorish woodwork, tile and carved plaster — mosque, madrasa and tomb gathered above the city, and a place of pilgrimage to this day.

Gharnati — the city’s music

Tlemcen is the Algerian guardian of gharnati, the strand of classical Andalusian music named for Granada. Performed in the city’s houses and festivals and taught across generations, it is the most living of Tlemcen’s links to al-Andalus.

Craft & identity

The city’s identity is urbane, devout and proud of its Andalusian inheritance, expressed in fine craft — embroidery, weaving and woodwork — as much as in its monuments. The El Mechouar palace-citadel in the centre, restored to its Zayyanid form, completes the picture of a royal Andalusian capital.

Beyond the monuments

Around the city, the Lalla Setti plateau, Tlemcen National Park and the El Ourit waterfalls give a green counterpoint to the heritage — see the Tlemcen travel guide to plan them into a visit, and the Tlemcen region guide for the wider west.

More to explore

highlands

Al Qal'a of Beni Hammad

Al Qal'a of Beni Hammad is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in the Hodna mountains of M'Sila state — the ruined first capital of the Hammadid dynasty, founded in 1007. Set at around 1,000 m, it preserves the largest mosque minaret in Algeria and the remains of palaces and fortifications, a vivid picture of a fortified Islamic city of the 11th century.
coast

The Casbah of Algiers

The Casbah of Algiers is the city's historic Ottoman citadel and old town — a steep maze of whitewashed houses, palaces, mosques and covered lanes overlooking the bay. A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1992, it is best explored on foot with a local guide.
highlands

Constantine

Constantine is the 'City of Bridges', a dramatic highland city in north-east Algeria built across a deep gorge above the Rhumel River and spanned by spectacular bridges. Ancient Cirta — a Numidian and Roman capital and later an Ottoman seat — it is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities on earth and the home of malouf music.

Frequently asked questions

What is Tlemcen known for?

Its deep Andalusian heritage — the 12th-century Great Mosque, the Mansourah minaret, the El Mechouar palace and the Sidi Boumediene complex — and as a centre of classical Andalusian (gharnati) music. It was a powerful medieval royal capital and is one of Algeria's most refined historic cities.

Why is Tlemcen called Andalusian, the Pearl of the Maghreb?

Waves of Muslim and Jewish refugees from Spain settled here, bringing the architecture, music, craft and learning of al-Andalus. That concentration of refined Hispano-Moorish culture, and the city's role as a capital of art and scholarship, earned it the name Pearl of the Maghreb.

What is gharnati music?

Gharnati is a classical Andalusian musical tradition — named for Granada — still performed and taught in Tlemcen, one of the city's living links to al-Andalus.

What are Tlemcen's great monuments?

The Almoravid Great Mosque, the lone Mansourah minaret and ramparts, the El Mechouar palace-citadel, and the Sidi Boumediene mosque, tomb and madrasa at El Eubbad.
