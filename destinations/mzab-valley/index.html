What it is

The M’Zab Valley is a cluster of five fortified desert towns around Ghardaïa, in the northern Algerian Sahara, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1982. Built from the 11th century by the Ibadi Mozabites — a distinct Amazigh Muslim community — the valley is a rare living example of a medieval urban plan still inhabited and used exactly as intended.

Why it matters

Each of the five towns is a small masterpiece of desert design: a tight spiral of houses climbing a hill to a mosque whose minaret doubles as a watchtower, ringed by walls, with palm groves and a sophisticated flood-water sharing system below. The whole was conceived as an egalitarian, climate-adapted community, and its logic so impressed modern architects that Le Corbusier studied it directly. Few places show so clearly how people built a humane city in one of the harshest environments on Earth.

What you’ll see

The pentapolis — Ghardaïa, Beni Isguen, Melika, Bounoura and El Atteuf — each with its hilltop mosque and pyramidal minaret. Ghardaïa’s arcaded market square, alive with carpets and craft, is the social heart; the holy walled town of Beni Isguen preserves its gates and customs and is visited with a local guide; El Atteuf holds the oldest mosque, Sidi Brahim, admired for its sculptural simplicity. Around them all stretch the palm groves that make desert life possible.

How to visit

Ghardaïa is the base, about 600 km south of Algiers and reachable by road or domestic flight. The cooler months, October to April, are the time to go. This is a conservative, religious community: dress modestly, ask before photographing people, and take a local guide, who is required for Beni Isguen. Al Qal'a of Beni Hammad

Al Qal'a of Beni Hammad is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in the Hodna mountains of M'Sila state — the ruined first capital of the Hammadid dynasty, founded in 1007. Set at around 1,000 m, it preserves the largest mosque minaret in Algeria and the remains of palaces and fortifications, a vivid picture of a fortified Islamic city of the 11th century.

Frequently asked questions

What is the M'Zab Valley?

A group of five fortified towns — a pentapolis — built from the 11th century by the Ibadi Mozabite community in a rocky Saharan valley around Ghardaïa. Each town climbs to a mosque whose minaret crowns the hill, surrounded by walls and palm groves. UNESCO inscribed the valley in 1982.

Which towns make up the M'Zab?

Ghardaïa, Beni Isguen, Melika, Bounoura and El Atteuf. Beni Isguen in particular is a holy walled town with its own visiting customs, best entered with a local guide.

Why is the M'Zab architecturally famous?

Its compact, egalitarian, climate-adapted design — and the way it organises water and shade in the desert — influenced modern architects, most famously Le Corbusier, who studied and admired it.

Can tourists visit the M'Zab?

Yes, with respect for a conservative, religious community: modest dress, asking before photographing people, and a local guide for the holy town of Beni Isguen. Ghardaïa is the base, reached by road or domestic flight.
